Calculation: 0.04/pass yard, 4/pass TD, −1/INT and 0.1/rush yard. Those rules reproduce **10.445 points/game, rounded to 10.45**. PPR alone does not define QB scoring; this is an explicit calculation, not a verified private-league setting.

**Observed trend:** completion rate and yards per attempt improved through the three full starts, with rushing contributing substantially in the latest game. Minnesota reports 40/48 passing for 364 yards over his last six quarters. [Vikings preview](https://www.vikings.com/news/indianapolis-colts-game-preview-nfl-week-7-2026).

Interpretation: improving comfort and accuracy are evident statistically; sustained explosive passing remains unproven. This assessment uses published statistics and reporting, not an independent film chart.

## 3. What Flores asks Jones to solve

[Cody Alexander's August 31 scheme breakdown](https://www.matchquarters.com/p/brian-flores-vikings-all-up-hawk-coverage) describes Flores' established use of:

- **Double-mug/all-up presentations:** crowd the line and threaten interior gaps to force protection decisions.
- **Simulated pressures:** threaten a larger rush, then drop defenders and change the rush's origin.
- **Hot/Hawk zone concepts:** pair pressure with coverage that reads the quarterback and attacks quick throws.

This is schematic background, including prior-season examples—not a chart of every 2026 call or a prediction of Sunday's exact package.

The relevant problem for Jones is recognizing both the rush and the coverage after the snap. A crowded front need not become an all-out blitz.

## 4. Jones against the blitz: a mixed result

The [Colts' October 23 preview](https://www.colts.com/news/colts-week-7-preview-brian-flores-aggressive-vikings-defense-presents-major-challenge-for-daniel-jones-shane-steichen) reports:

- Indianapolis ranks ninth in EPA/play against blitzes.
- None of Jones' six interceptions came against blitzes.
- Jones averages 5.5 yards/attempt against blitzes.
- He faces pressure on 48.7% of blitzed dropbacks.
- Indianapolis' explosive passing rate against blitzes is 6.6%, second-lowest.

Avoiding interceptions helps the aggregate efficiency result; these numbers do not establish consistent downfield punishment of pressure.

[Locked On Vikings analysis summarized by Athlon](https://athlonsports.com/nfl/minnesota-vikings/vikings-brian-flores-blitz-daniel-jones-colts), attributing the split to TruMedia, says Jones has the third-lowest target depth when blitzes are blocked without producing pressure. This is a secondary report, not a directly retrieved TruMedia dataset.

**Matchup inference:** Flores may induce checkdowns and trust pursuit/tackling to prevent sustained drives without requiring sacks on every series.

**Source discrepancy:** Hribar reports a 66.5% Minnesota blitz rate and 5.2 yards/attempt for Jones against blitzes; the Colts preview reports 77.8% and 5.5 respectively. Definitions/denominators were not reconciled. Do not combine them into one supposedly settled measure. Both portray frequent Minnesota pressure and limited Jones passing gains against blitzes.

## 5. How strong is the Colts offense?

Indianapolis scores 24 team points/game, 14th, but ranks **29th in passing EPA and third in rushing EPA**. Team scoring includes contributions beyond offensive efficiency. [Hribar worksheet](https://www.sharpfootballanalysis.com/fantasy/colts-vikings-week-7-fantasy-football-preview-nfl-worksheet-rich-hribar-2026/amp/).
