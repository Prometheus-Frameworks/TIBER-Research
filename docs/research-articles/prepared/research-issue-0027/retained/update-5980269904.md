### 2. DARKO principles that transfer, and those that do not

[DARKO's own overview](https://www.darko.app/about) documents sequential component forecasts, stat-specific decay, shrinkage/prior machinery, modified Kalman estimates combined with boosted trees, and contextual updating. Transfer the testable ideas: learn how quickly different signals change, pool sparse evidence, and update consistently as new information arrives.

Do not transfer NBA possession/on-off structure, DPM scale, blend weights, decay rates, aging curves or rookie assumptions. NFL weekly samples, byes, injury-related absences and discontinuous roles differ materially. The public overview is not a complete reproducible specification of all likelihoods, transition assumptions or historical model vintages.

Role → opportunity → efficiency → fantasy production is a candidate factorization, not an established identity. Efficiency is conditional on selected opportunities and context. Multiplying independent marginal means ignores dependence; a coherent joint model must compete against direct prediction.

### 3. Evidence gaps

Before empirical work, establish:
1. Historical eligibility/population and source versions available by each forecast cutoff
2. Stable source identity, event teams, credited-target definitions, exposures and correction handling
3. Enough compatible seasons for chronological tuning and an untouched later evaluation period
4. Permitted historical injury/return, team and depth-chart witnesses for event-specific claims
5. Source-purpose rights and a consumer representation that can honestly withhold unsupported uncertainty

Missing records, byes, inactive status and genuine zero opportunities are distinct. Routes and snaps cannot be inferred from targets. Current source-backed historical evidence and reviewed W1/W2 packets are useful, but do not automatically supply every needed field or Forecast-purpose permission.

A new algorithm may be evaluated by honest **pseudo-real-time walk-forward retraining** using only eligible earlier inputs and reproducibly versioned code. It need not have existed historically. Archived model vintages/predictions are separately required when claiming an actual historically issued forecast or comparing against one. With only corrected logs and no availability witnesses, label the test restricted retrospective rather than deployment-equivalent.

### 4. Candidate state schema

A separate, proposed inferred checkpoint should carry:
- Immutable checkpoint/predecessor IDs; source-native identity; nullable canonical mapping with confidence and reason
- Event window, forecast target/horizon, decision cutoff, source availability/publication/retrieval and correction clocks
- Model, training cutoff, prior, feature-manifest and exact digest-profile versions
- Component records marked observed/inferred/unavailable, with units, denominators, exposure, evidence references and limitations
- Availability separate from participation and workload
- **Latent-state uncertainty, future-outcome variability and model/data limitations separately**
- Prior/current estimates and numerical update attribution or sensitivity, explicitly not causal attribution
- Immutable correction forks/supersession; purpose/privacy/consumer eligibility; withholding and stale-display rules
- Scoring-rule version only if a fantasy distribution is actually supported

No universal score, implied role security, fabricated interval or newly fresh timestamp from an absent source. Existing digest profiles and card/publication pathways are distinct and require explicit integration; a numeric-range card must not force invented ranges into point-only evidence.

### 5. Architecture options

| Option | Mechanism and benefit | Main cost or failure risk |
|---|---|---|
| A. Discounted counts/exposures with shrinkage | Transparent sufficient-statistic updates, a few train-only decay/prior parameters, position-aware pooling | Can lag discontinuities; equal weighting of noisy percentages misstates information; EWM alone is not a predictive distribution |
| B. Hierarchical dynamic/state-space model | Evolving player/team states with suitable observation distributions and separate process/measurement uncertainty | Sparse histories may not identify latent change versus noise; Gaussian assumptions can be poor for counts, shares and nonparticipation; smoothing leaks future information |
| C. Pooled learned distributional model | Nonlinear context/features with explicit pooling and calibrated outcome distributions | Greater data/tuning burden, cold-start fragility, identity memorization and backfilled-feature leakage; feature importance is not causality |

Prefer A as the first candidate. B and C must earn their complexity. QB starting/pass/rush structure, RB carry/receiving allocation, and WR/TE participation/target allocation need position-specific treatment. Rookies require tested priors; offseason inactivity must not be encoded as poor play.

### 6. Evaluation protocol

Freeze population, target, horizon, cutoff, source-purpose scope, correction policy and holdout before outcome inspection. Fit priors, decays, normalizers, pooling and calibration only inside earlier chronological folds. Filter forward, never use full-series smoothed states as forecasts. Preserve pre-cutoff eligibility, including low-history players and true zeros. [Rolling-origin evaluation](https://otexts.com/fpp3/tscv.html)

Compare shrunk season-to-date, predeclared rolling windows, direct EWM, eligible existing Forecast outputs and admissible timestamped consensus where available. Ablate allocation, Teamstate, efficiency and event context separately.

Predeclare a proper distributional score such as count log loss or CRPS; report MAE/RMSE, interval coverage **and width**, calibration, position/experience/event cohorts and sample counts. Use dependence-aware paired uncertainty and season robustness. Choose practical improvement thresholds before the final evaluation. [Proper scoring rules](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/people/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf)

Test responsiveness to documented pre-cutoff starter absences/returns or team changes. A long touchdown, unusual conversion rate, bye, missing record or after-cutoff information must not masquerade as a demonstrated persistent role change.

### 9. Smallest conditional offline experiment

After a separate exact-input authorization, forecast **next-game WR credited target counts** at one predeclared pre-week deadline:
1. Direct shrunk target-count EWM
2. Team target volume × allocation with shared decay
3. The same decomposition with separately learned volume/allocation decay

Use the full eligible population, coherent shares plus residual categories, consistent numerator/exposure discounting and a suitable count distribution. Do not fit free player-specific half-lives initially.

Falsifiable tests: decomposition must improve on direct EWM; separate decay must improve on shared decay; pooling should help sparse histories; any event adjustment must improve the predeclared event subset without unacceptable overall harm. No improvement or inconclusive evidence stops added complexity. A target-count gain does not validate fantasy-point distributions; those require a later direct-versus-decomposed test.
