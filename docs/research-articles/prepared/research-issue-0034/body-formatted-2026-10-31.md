### 2. Proposed representation — a profile, not one number

Use position-specific traits supported by three complementary evidence types:

1. **Movement:** the measurable action, such as acceleration, braking, redirection or body adjustment.
2. **Execution:** performance of the football task, with timing, technique and assignment limitations made explicit.
3. **Competitive result:** the advantage or disruption that emerged relative to a declared comparison.

Do not average these evidence types as though they were independent measurements. Shared plays, source lineage and derived quantities must remain identifiable.

| Candidate domain | Question | Candidate evidence; not established TIBER fields |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RB creation | Does the runner improve the opportunity through decisions, movement and contact management? | Situation-adjusted rushing outcomes, assessable tackle encounters, movement through available space; receiving evaluated separately |
| WR separation creation — first candidate | Does the receiver create a useful receiving window through movement and execution? | Route-phase movement, defender-relative advantage, release/break execution and window timing |
| WR finishing | Does the receiver convert difficult, assessable receiving opportunities? | Ball placement, boundary and defender context, adjustment and catch completion, including failures |
| WR after-catch creation | What advantage emerges after possession? | Catch-point context, pursuit geometry, movement and subsequent outcomes |
| Edge disruption | How consistently and quickly does the defender defeat resistance or disrupt the intended action? | Qualifying rush reps, blocking conditions, time to pressure and disruption; sacks are a separate outcome |

For later interpretations of exceptional ability, preserve **rarity within a defined comparison group, breadth across tasks, and repeatability across conditions**. These are proposed analytical dimensions, not adopted composite weights. Equal percentiles across different traits or positions do not imply equal football value.

Opponent response, such as extra blocking or coverage attention, should be a separate contextual view. It is not an automatic ability bonus or independent proof of causal impact.

### 3. Bounded first study: WR separation creation

<!-- source-block 6; retrospective organizational mapping, not an original question ID -->
### Define the task before selecting a formula

The candidate unit is an **assessable receiver route or route segment**. Codex should recommend and justify one narrow route family/phase for the smallest feasible test, rather than combine every route immediately.

The design must specify:
- Route/segment start and end, frame/event alignment, outcome timing, and treatment of multiple phases on one route.
- What constitutes a useful window or route win. Geometric distance to the nearest defender alone must not silently become a complete football-execution grade.
- Route family/depth, alignment, motion, press/off context, defender leverage, relevant coverage, field/boundary position and timing where actually evidenced.
- Treatment of option routes, unknown assignments, scrambles, early play termination, penalties/no-plays and incomplete visibility.
- Inclusion of non-targeted routes where assessable; no target-only or highlight-only sample presented as all-route ability.
- A reproducible eligible population and comparison cohort, not only named stars or players selected for their subsequent success.

Potential movement descriptors include speed entering a comparable break, speed loss while redirecting, reacceleration, and change in defender-relative advantage. A sharper cut or higher maximum speed is not automatically better route execution. Measurement resolution, coordinate orientation, smoothing, event detection and derivative noise require an explicit feasibility check; do not assume the available tracking can resolve every proposed movement trait.

Missing, unassessable, not tested and tested unsuccessfully are different states. If only targeted routes or aggregate summaries are available, name the narrower estimand and selection limitations rather than manufacturing full-route coverage.

<!-- source-block 7; retrospective organizational mapping, not an original question ID -->
### Avoid adjusting away the trait

For each candidate feature, identify whether it represents the initial task/context, the execution being assessed, an intermediate effect of that execution, or the final outcome.

**Separation may be context for a catching assessment but is partly the outcome for a route-creation assessment.** Similarly, burst must not be treated only as a nuisance control when burst is the trait of interest. Declare the timing and attribution boundary before adjustment.

Scheme, coverage, QB timing, teammate routes and defensive attention can remain confounded with individual execution. Limited overlap between players' tasks limits portability claims. A useful adjusted association is not automatically isolated player causation.

### 4. Candidate modeling approach — to compare, not implement now

Begin with transparent measurements and reference comparisons. A generic idea is:

`execution residual = observed task outcome - expected task outcome under declared comparable conditions`

For a binary task outcome, `sum(y - p_expected) / N_eligible` is an illustrative average residual, not a selected WR metric or proof of causal credit. The expected-outcome model must itself be evaluated; omitted context and model errors can remain in the residual.

Compare the smallest credible candidates:
- **B0: context-only/reference baseline**, with no inferred player-ability state.
- **B1: simple historical task-performance estimate with pooling/shrinkage**, alongside an unadjusted baseline under the same sample and cutoff.
- **B2: narrowly enriched movement/execution estimate**, only if compatible evidence exists and the extra complexity can be tested.

A later persistent trait estimate may use partial pooling and sequential updating. Learn pooling/update/decay choices only from training evidence; do not hand-select weights to make familiar names rank highly. Reconcile Research #27 and Forecast's existing program before proposing another estimator.

Separate latent-state uncertainty, future rep/outcome variability, and data/model limitations. Do not invent calibrated intervals, qualitative elite labels or decimal precision merely to populate a card.

### 6. Validation and falsification design

Before any later empirical execution, freeze the population, unit, task/outcome definition, source versions, cutoff, exclusions, baseline, practical improvement criterion and untouched evaluation period.

Required design considerations:
- Chronological held-out evaluation on later reps/games under a clearly declared retrospective or point-in-time scope. Corrected historical data without availability witnesses must not be described as an as-known-then backtest.
- Comparable samples and information boundaries across baselines/candidates; fit normalizers, thresholds, priors and calibration only on training folds.
- Avoid route/frame pseudoreplication; account for within-play, player, game and team dependence as appropriate. Propose a sample/precision justification rather than an arbitrary minimum.
- Evaluate persistence of the same football task, measurement/rubric reliability, coverage/abstention and sensitivity to deployment, coverage and opponent mix.
- Where manual charting is needed, predefine the rubric and independently double-review a subset. Mask player/reputation and outcome information where feasible; disclose when blinding is impossible. Reviewer agreement is not empirical ground truth.
- A label derived from the same inputs as the score is not independent validation. Distinguish reproduction of a measurement from prediction of later task execution and from construct validity.
- Include sparse-history players, failures, null findings, missingness and adverse cases. Withhold strong portability or cross-context claims when task overlap is insufficient.
- Compare B1 against B0/simple history, and B2 against B1. No material or reliable gain, unstable labels, insufficient sample, or unacceptable selection bias are valid reasons to narrow or stop.

Better future fantasy-point prediction alone would not validate a raw-ability construct; it may reflect role or environment. Conversely, a descriptive execution profile must not be marketed as a fantasy forecasting improvement without that separate test.
