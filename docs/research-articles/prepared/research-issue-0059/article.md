### Prospective rebound questions

#### Nico Collins — target concentration or a one-game conversion failure?

1. **C1 — What actually produced the five-target game?** Reconstruct Week 7 snaps, routes, targets per route and share of credited team targets against his Weeks 1–6 baseline. Did he lose route access, receive fewer targets despite stable routes, or fail to convert the opportunities he received?
2. **C2 — How did Houston distribute opportunities?** Compare Collins with Dell, Harrison, Noel and the other actual participants. Inspect ordinary possessions, downfield and red-zone opportunities, and target quality where available. Separate competition for work from team passing volume; do not infer a coverage matchup from the box score.
3. **C3 — What changed during the bye that could alter his next game?** Record substantiated health, personnel or deployment changes and distinguish coach intentions from observed deployment. An uneventful bye leaves the performance question unresolved.
4. **C4 — Deferred to Week 9:** Does his route access and target concentration return toward his own earlier baseline, with usable opportunities across drives? A long TD on another low-volume day would improve the score without necessarily resolving the role concern.

#### Brock Purdy — turnover/conversion outlier or a weakened passing environment?

1. **P1 — What can we establish about the two interceptions?** Review the actual plays when suitable evidence exists: decision, accuracy, receiver interaction, pressure or deflection. Keep ambiguous responsibility unresolved; INT counts alone do not identify the mechanism.
2. **P2 — Why did 40 attempts and 288 yards produce only one passing TD?** Inspect scoring possessions, red-zone dropbacks, passing versus rushing TD distribution, sacks and stalled drives. Was the issue access to scoring chances or conversion after getting them?
3. **P3 — Does the bye materially improve the supporting cast?** Identify which receivers, blockers or other skill players were actually missing/limited, the evidence for recovery, and remaining uncertainty. Do not count projected returns as completed repairs.
4. **P4 — Deferred to Week 9:** With the actual available cast, do pressure handling, passing efficiency and sustained scoring drives improve? Separate improved execution from an easier opponent or a small number of favorable TD outcomes.

#### Derrick Henry — does a better environment restore useful rushing?

1. **H1 — Did Jackson actually return, and under what conditions?** Record QB participation, restrictions and offensive-line starters. If Huntley starts again or the line changes materially, evaluate that branch instead of judging the unobserved Jackson-return hypothesis.
2. **H2 — Is Henry's valuable workload intact?** Compare his share of RB carries, snaps and passing work, plus carries inside the 10 and inside the 5, with team opportunities and the other backs. Record QB goal-line work separately. A high carry total can coexist with reduced scoring access.
3. **H3 — Does the rushing distribution improve?** Preserve all carries and compare zero/negative gains, successful gains and dependence on a longest run. Add yards before/after contact or missed tackles only with consistent source definitions. Distinguish improved blocking access from identifiable runner creation; raw YPC cannot settle either.
4. **H4 — Is any rebound supported beyond touchdowns?** Assess drive quality, field position, scoring chances and competitive-score carries. Two short TDs with continued difficulty gaining yards would be a fantasy rebound with a partially unresolved rushing problem; improved process without a TD can still be encouraging.

#### Patrick Mahomes — matchup interruption or a recurring offensive limitation?

1. **M1 — What receiving and protection resources were actually available?** Record Rice/Moore status, workload limits and actual usage of the active receivers. Does the passing structure change, and is any improvement contingent on a returning player?
2. **M2 — Does the offense sustain passing production across possessions?** Compare yards per attempt, completion results, sacks, third-down outcomes and downfield gains using full-game evidence. Add pressure-specific measures only if available; fewer sacks alone do not prove less pressure.
3. **M3 — Do scoring opportunities recover?** Track drives reaching the red zone, TD conversion and passing versus rushing finishes. A Walker rushing TD can improve KC's offense without providing Mahomes a fantasy rebound.
4. **M4 — What evidence persists beyond one explosive or late drive?** Inspect when productive plays occur and who receives the opportunities. Continued stalled drives with similar protection/receiver constraints would sustain concern; broader productive passing would weaken the simple decline hypothesis without settling ROS value.

#### Bryce Young — short-week repair or recurring breakdown?

1. **Y1 — What protection did Carolina actually field at Green Bay?** Verify Moton's status, replacements, other material absences and any documented changes in protection or pass design. Do not attribute every sack to the line or every pressure to Young.
2. **Y2 — Do the turnover mechanisms recur?** Review interceptions, fumbles and sacks with play context where possible. Distinguish observable execution from claims about intended reads or assignments. Zero interceptions alone would not prove decision-making repaired.
3. **Y3 — Is production spread across sustainable drives?** Compare series, first downs, passing efficiency and scoring opportunities rather than leaning on one long TD. Preserve the complete passing line and note whether explosives or late possessions dominate it.
4. **Y4 — What happens while the game remains competitive, and what happens afterward?** Record score/clock context without deleting late plays. Trailing can increase pass attempts, while sacks, turnovers and opponent possession can reduce usable drives; a blowout is not automatically an opportunity excuse. Record any substantiated coaching or job-security development separately from speculation.
