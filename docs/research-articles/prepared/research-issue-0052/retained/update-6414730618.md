## Week 7 postgame answers — October 26, 2026

**Applicable branch: A — Puka played.** Rams won 35–21 at Las Vegas on October 25. This is an appended assessment of our preserved pregame questions; the original evidence and both prospective branches remain intact.

**New evidence since the immediate postgame review:** Pro Football Reference's dated player rows report **31 offensive snaps / 43.1% for Mumpfield** and **61 / 84.7% for Nacua**. These are offensive snaps, not routes. Mumpfield's Week 6 share on the same provider was 27 / 46.6%; his share did not expand in Week 7. Provider-reported snap figures have not been independently reconciled against the official gamebook.

### Branch A answers

**A1. Did Puka participate normally, or did restrictions/an early exit change the environment?**

**Meaningful participation supported; specific injury restriction unresolved.** Puka was active, recorded 8 catches for 68 yards and a TD on 10 targets, and added 2 carries for 10 yards. Yahoo's play-by-play records a fourth-quarter carry at 12:02 and catch at 11:20. PFR reports 84.7% of offensive snaps, versus 89.7% in Week 6. This supports a substantial role extending into the fourth quarter. It does not establish whether coaches imposed a calf-related restriction or why any particular snap was missed. We have not verified a material injury-driven early exit.

**A2. With Adams absent, did Mumpfield secure a regular route role alongside Puka?**

**Expanded offensive participation challenged; regular route role unresolved.** PFR reports 31 offensive snaps (43.1%), compared with 27 (46.6%) in Week 6 and 63 (73.3%) in Week 3. He remained involved, but the available snap evidence does not show a larger share after Adams' absence. We cannot substitute offensive snaps for routes: Week 7 routes, route participation, alignment and personnel splits remain unavailable in this review.

**A3. Did he earn routine early-down/progression targets, or occasional schemed/late-game/coverage-spillover looks?**

**One routine early-down contribution supported; the broader mechanism unresolved.** His 15-yard catch came on second-and-10 at the Raiders' 27 with 12:19 left in the second quarter. That was a first-down conversion during a competitive phase, not a late consolation catch. He finished with only two targets. We have not verified the other target's route/coverage outcome, first-read status, progression placement or whether either opportunity was specifically designed. One early-down catch does not establish recurring integration across drives.

**A5. Did releases/breaks/timing create usable passing windows against the relevant coverages?**

**Unresolved.** We have not independently charted film or retrieved credible Week 7 separation, man/zone, press/off, route-concept or coverage-attention splits. The catch provides evidence of a completed first-down opportunity, not a complete separation assessment. Two targets cannot tell us whether he failed to separate, ran clear-out routes, was outside the progression, encountered pressure-related missed windows or simply was not selected. His uncaught target is not established as a drop.

**A6. What supports or weakens a lasting expanded role with Puka on the field?**

**The immediate expansion hypothesis is challenged; long-term skill and role remain unresolved.** A 43.1% offensive snap share and two targets do not support a newly established high-volume receiving role. Puka and the tight ends commanded most recorded passing opportunities. The ordinary second-down contribution and continued offensive participation leave room for development, but do not establish a dependable fantasy role. Our earlier separation evidence remains a skill hypothesis requiring route-level evaluation; this game does not refute it.

Williams and Corum combined for 192 rushing yards on 33 carries. No Rams QB replacement appears in the final passing statistics. Week 7's 2 targets, 1 catch, 15 yards, 0 TD and 43.1% snaps do not reproduce that opportunity. This is not a controlled like-for-like comparison.
