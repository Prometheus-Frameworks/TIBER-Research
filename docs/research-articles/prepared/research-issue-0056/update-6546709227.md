##### 1. Reconciled outcomes and a common opportunity denominator

Miami recorded **14 completions, 21 pass attempts, 208 passing yards and two passing touchdowns**, with 41 official rushing attempts. The official Jets box and Miami recap agree on the receiving production. ESPN supplies targets. [1–3]

All target percentages below are **targets divided by Miami's 21 pass attempts**. All snap percentages use the **65-offensive-snap convention** reported by Dolphins Wire and Pro Football Reference; snaps include a different play universe from official counted plays. These are separate measurements. [2, 4, 5]

| Receiver | Offensive snaps / 65 | Targets / 21 attempts | Receptions–yards–TD | Rushing |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | --- |
| Caleb Douglas | 44 / 65 = 67.7% | 3 / 21 = 14.3% | 2–70–2 | 0 carries |
| Chris Bell | 31 / 65 = 47.7% | 4 / 21 = 19.0% | 3–25–0 | 0 carries |
| Malik Washington | 52 / 65 = 80.0% | 3 / 21 = 14.3% | 3–27–0 | 2 carries, 8 yards |
| Kevin Coleman Jr. | 10 / 65 = 15.4% | 1 / 21 = 4.8% | 0–0–0 | 0 carries |

All four recorded offensive snaps, establishing actual participation; a medically managed workload was not verified. The four receivers account for **11 targets**. Dulcich had five, Gordon three, Wright one and Traore one, completing the 21-target allocation. Dulcich led the entire team. The complete receiving ledger sums to 14 catches and 208 yards. [1, 2]

Douglas's 63-yard touchdown supplied **90.0% of his receiving yards** and **30.3% of Miami's passing yards**. Both catches scored. These calculated concentration measures preserve the successful plays while limiting what their fantasy result says about recurring opportunity.

**Measurement qualification:** The play log supports **28 counted pass-origin outcomes: 21 attempts + two sacks + five labelled scrambles**, excluding the erased Washington pass described below. That matches RotoWire's dropback total. PFF's exposed team total is 26; its inclusion rules were not established here. RotoWire also uses 64 team plays rather than the 65-snap table above. Neither difference is silently normalized.

The PFF embed additionally displays a stale preseason caption and a Willis rushing line of 9/33 that disagrees with the verified 8/37. Its routes remain attributed chart evidence, not independently validated tracking. RotoWire attributes its statistics generally to NFL Next Gen; its underlying play-level route records were not available to us. Its final italicized columns are season rates, which were not substituted for weekly values.

**What survives both versions:** Washington had the most routes, Douglas the next most, Bell a meaningful passing role, and Coleman only two. Bell received more targets than Douglas or Washington on fewer reported routes. Coleman's 50% rate describes **one target on two routes** and cannot establish strong sustainable target earning. An exact claim that Douglas ran “over 80% of all dropbacks” is not reconciled.



##### 4. Deployment, situations and film leads

**Personnel evidence, source-reported:** Jahnke describes Douglas and Washington as the primary pair, Bell as the clear third receiver, and five of Coleman's ten snaps occurring in single-WR sets. He also reports Wayne playing more than Coleman in both 11 and 12 personnel. [6]

Donabedian's two-WR formation figures are Washington 83%, Douglas 76% and Bell 21%. His 11-personnel figures are Washington 100%, Bell 93% and Douglas 78%. These support broader package access for Douglas alongside Washington, with Bell more dependent on three-WR packages. They do not measure slot/outside alignment. [8]

**Play-log checks:** All 21 counted targets were reconciled against the receiving box; selected events below retain situational context. ESPN supplies the log, with Bell's sequence also checked in PFR. [3, 5]

- Douglas's opening touchdown was **Q1 10:53, third-and-goal from the 7**. He also received a **Q4 8:03 third-and-8 incompletion** before the 63-yard touchdown at **Q4 4:44, second-and-7**.
- Bell received **three third-down targets**: Q2 4:02, an eight-yard catch on third-and-10; Q3 8:40, an incompletion on third-and-5; Q3 4:15, a nine-yard conversion on third-and-7. His other catch came on first down. This is meaningful situational involvement; only one of these three targets converted. [5]
- Washington's three catches occurred before halftime. A **Q2 0:20 goal-to-go pass intended for him drew defensive pass interference and was marked “No Play.”** Preserve that additional receiving opportunity separately; it is not a fourth official target. His two official carries also preserve a rushing-touch channel.
- Coleman's sole counted target was an early **Q1 7:22** incompletion. His limited involvement was not confined to a late-game appearance.

The long Douglas score is labelled a **short pass** in the play log. Its 63 receiving yards cannot be treated as 63 air yards. Exact catch depth and YAC were not independently measured here.

**Attributed film observations:** Tyler Carmona's October 27 report describes Douglas winning on an in-breaking goal-line route and Bell receiving shallow-crossing work from mesh concepts. His QB discussion describes Douglas being found later in the progression on the first touchdown. These provide relevant leads beyond a purely vertical-role explanation; they are the writer's interpretations, not our independent read-order or separation chart. [11]

The official highlights and film-review podcast pages were located, but retrieval supplied page descriptions rather than usable footage or a full transcript. No independent All-22 review, representative untargeted-route sample or completed Bell erased-play ledger is claimed. [12, 13]



## 5. Interpretation and assessment

##### 3. What changed from the last pre-bye game?

Week 5 against Cincinnati is a dated comparison, not a substitute for a complete Weeks 1–5 study. Miami had **27 attempts** then, versus 21 in Week 7; its snap table had 66 offensive snaps, versus 65. Week 6 was a bye, not a zero-opportunity game. ESPN and PFR agree on these Week 5 receiver targets. [9, 10]

| Receiver | Week 5 → Week 7 offensive snap share | Week 5 → Week 7 targets / team attempts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Douglas | Out → 44/65 (67.7%) | Out → 3/21 (14.3%) |
| Bell | 47/66 (71.2%) → 31/65 (47.7%) | 2/27 (7.4%) → 4/21 (19.0%) |
| Washington | 53/66 (80.3%) → 52/65 (80.0%) | 8/27 (29.6%) → 3/21 (14.3%) |
| Coleman | 42/66 (63.6%) → 10/65 (15.4%) | 7/27 (25.9%) → 1/21 (4.8%) |

- **Coleman:** The loss of snaps and reported route access goes well beyond the reduction in team attempts. This is the strongest evidence of displacement within the receiving rotation.
- **Bell:** His total playing-time share fell, but his target count and targets per team attempt increased. “Douglas returned, therefore Bell stopped getting opportunities” fails this game's target evidence.
- **Washington:** His on-field access persisted. His target rate fell substantially as well as his raw target count, so lower passing volume alone does not describe the entire change.
- **Douglas:** The substantial-return part of the pregame question is supported. A full pre-injury workload, recurring first-read priority and a dominant target share are separate propositions that remain unestablished.

These are observed changes and interpretations of them. A single before/after comparison cannot identify how many opportunities Douglas personally caused each teammate to lose.



## 6. Unknowns and revision triggers

##### 5. Alternative explanations and unresolved measurements

Douglas's return coincided with a materially different game. Miami led 21–7 at halftime, ran 41 times and held the ball for 34:36. A winning, run-heavy script can reduce three-WR exposure and raw targets. [1, 4] The bye also brought Wayne into the room; PFF reports Tolbert as a healthy inactive. [6] Personnel selection, opponent coverage, protection, Willis's decisions and scrambles can all coexist with receiver competition.

Consequently:

- **Supported:** a substantial Douglas return; a clear Coleman access contraction; retained Washington playing-time priority; continued Bell target involvement despite fewer total snaps.
- **Not established:** Douglas as the sustained target leader; Bell failing to separate; Washington losing his underlying place in the rotation; Coleman permanently displaced specifically by Douglas.
- **Still unavailable or unreconciled:** a complete same-definition Weeks 1–5 route series; exact Week 7 cross-provider route reconciliation; full slot/outside/motion splits; route trees and win rates; independently charted first reads, catchability, coverage/pressure assignments, air yards and YAC; complete red-zone/third-down *route* denominators; and any causal attribution of the redistribution. Absence of these measurements is not a zero.

**Source-quality notes:** Miami's recap gives 4:55 remaining for the long touchdown; ESPN's play log and PFR's scoring table give 4:44. The event is secure; this update uses the matching ESPN play-log/PFR scoring timestamp. PFR's Bell play-log clocks also differ from ESPN by one or two seconds; the down/distance, sequence and gains agree. The clocks above use ESPN. Carmona's article also contains an inconsistent statement about Dulcich's Cincinnati target history; the verified Week 5 box gives him five targets. That sentence is not used as evidence.
