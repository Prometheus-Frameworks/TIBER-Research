## Questions to answer after the game

1. **Does Jeanty get more room?** Track contact behind the line, yards before contact, and the share of carries gaining zero or negative yards. Where available, examine blocking execution, run direction, box counts and replacement defenders. Do not infer blocking fault from a negative gain alone.
2. **What does he create with that room?** Examine missed tackles forced, yards after contact, decisions on identifiable lanes, and the distribution of gains. Report the full rushing line and whether the average depends heavily on one long run. Preserve ambiguous attribution.
3. **Does his role recover or change?** Record snaps, carries, routes/targets, passing-down work, and goal-line opportunities for Jeanty and the other backs. Washington being inactive does not establish that every vacated opportunity goes to Jeanty.
4. **How much does game state explain?** Separate competitive-score carries from late-game situations, record score/down/distance where feasible, and distinguish rushing process from touchdowns and the final fantasy total.

Use Weeks 1–6 as a baseline with consistent source definitions and denominators. Advanced rushing metrics have not yet been collected for this issue; unavailable measurements remain unknown.

## How findings would update the thesis

- **More access plus consistent productive carries:** supports the proposed environment mechanism for this matchup; one game does not establish a lasting turnaround.
- **More access but continued struggles:** challenges an explanation based only on poor surroundings; inspect runner decisions and execution.
- **Continued immediate disruption:** documents another poor outcome while leaving the contribution of runner versus environment unresolved.
- **A strong fantasy score driven by touchdowns or one explosive:** record the result separately from evidence of repeatable rushing improvement.

## October 24 pregame addition — receiving opportunity and creation after the catch

**Research question:** With Jeanty drawing targets at a high rate, are we also seeing evidence of elite or high-upside skill in beating defenders after receptions? Keep receiving opportunity, tackle breaking, route separation and fantasy output as separate questions.

### Opportunity signal supplied in our discussion

[Mark Dankenbring's October 23 post](https://x.com/MarkDank/status/2113760581836509516), supplied as a screenshot, reports Jeanty leading qualifying RBs through Week 6 at **29.8% targets per route run, with 37 targets**. The post attributes its data to SIS, uses a minimum of 75 routes, and excludes Week 7 NE/CHI Thursday Night Football.

This is a strong reported involvement signal. It does not by itself establish why he is being targeted or how much yardage he creates independently.

The rate counts missed tackles per catch, not the percentage of receptions on which a defender was beaten, and does not adjust for the number or quality of tackle opportunities.

Jeanty's **two receiving missed tackles rank 23rd on PFF's RB table**; that is the displayed count rank, not a calculated per-reception rank. His public page was last updated October 23 and separately lists nine rushing missed tackles; those nine are not used here.

**Provider boundary:** PFF lists 35 targets and 134 routes for Jeanty, while the SIS-attributed post reports 37 targets and a 29.8% target rate. We have not reconciled that difference. Preserve both source definitions and do not combine their target/route denominators.

### Other evidence relevant to receiving upside

- **Observed after-catch production:** [FOX's 2026 receiving table](https://www.foxsports.com/nfl/stats?category=receiving&sort=rec_yac&season=2026&seasonType=reg&sortOrder=desc), checked October 24, lists Jeanty with six games, 26 receptions, 251 receiving yards and **214 yards after catch**. That is **8.23 YAC per reception**, calculated as 214 / 26. Raw YAC can reflect open space, blocking and play design as well as individual creation; it is not YAC over expected.
- **Historical receiving capability:** [PFF's October 4, 2024 college analysis](https://www.pff.com/news/college-football-ashton-jeanty-boise-state-broncos-ridiculous-numbers-2024) reports that Jeanty's **91.6 receiving grade in 2023 led FBS running backs**. This supports a historical receiving-upside hypothesis; it is neither an NFL result nor a receiving missed-tackle measurement.

**Our current interpretation:** The receiving-only missed-tackle evidence does not establish elite NFL tackle breaking so far. The reported target involvement, substantial YAC production and historical receiving profile keep receiving upside plausible. Six games and 26 catches do not settle his underlying ability. These data also do not establish elite route separation or show how often he defeats the first available defender.

### Receiving questions to answer against the Rams

1. **Does the opportunity persist?** Record routes, targets, target rate using one provider's consistent definitions, passing-down work and the other backs' usage. Keep Washington's active/inactive branches from the original intake.
2. **What is the complete receiving outcome?** Preserve targets, catches, receiving yards, touchdowns, drops where available, YAC and receiving-only missed tackles. Keep receiving MTF separate from rushing MTF and compare with the Weeks 1–6 baseline.
3. **When he catches with usable space, does he beat the first defender?** If we chart film, record catch location relative to the line, immediate pressure, available space, blocking, the first identifiable tackle opportunity, the outcome and uncertainty. Retain every reception; an access-conditioned slice must not replace the full receiving result.
4. **How is he creating yards?** Distinguish identifiable contact balance, broken tackles, changes of direction, acceleration or angle manipulation from yards supplied by open grass and blocking. Preserve mixed or ambiguous attribution. Assess route separation before the catch separately.
5. **Is the evidence repeatable?** Inspect the distribution of gains and whether one explosive, touchdown or broken coverage drives the result. Add YAC over expected only if a correctly scoped source is available; otherwise leave it unknown.
