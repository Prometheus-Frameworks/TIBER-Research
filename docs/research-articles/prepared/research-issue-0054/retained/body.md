## Questions to answer after the game

1. **Does Jeanty get more room?** Track contact behind the line, yards before contact, and the share of carries gaining zero or negative yards. Where available, examine blocking execution, run direction, box counts and replacement defenders. Do not infer blocking fault from a negative gain alone.
2. **What does he create with that room?** Examine missed tackles forced, yards after contact, decisions on identifiable lanes, and the distribution of gains. Report the full rushing line and whether the average depends heavily on one long run. Preserve ambiguous attribution.
3. **Does his role recover or change?** Record snaps, carries, routes/targets, passing-down work, and goal-line opportunities for Jeanty and the other backs. Washington being inactive does not establish that every vacated opportunity goes to Jeanty.
4. **How much does game state explain?** Separate competitive-score carries from late-game situations, record score/down/distance where feasible, and distinguish rushing process from touchdowns and the final fantasy total.

Use Weeks 1–6 as a baseline with consistent source definitions and denominators. Advanced rushing metrics have not yet been collected for this issue; unavailable measurements remain unknown.

## How findings would update the thesis

- **More access plus consistent productive carries:** supports the proposed environment mechanism for this matchup; one game does not establish a lasting turnaround.
- **More access but continued struggles:** challenges an explanation based only on poor surroundings; inspect runner decisions and execution.
- **Continued immediate disruption:** documents another poor outcome while leaving the contribution of runner versus environment unresolved.
- **A strong fantasy score driven by touchdowns or one explosive:** record the result separately from evidence of repeatable rushing improvement.
