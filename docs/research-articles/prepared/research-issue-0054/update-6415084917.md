##### 1. Verified outcomes and usage

The [official Raiders box score][1], [FootballDB box score][2] and [PFR game page][3] agree on the principal player lines:

| Player | Carries | Rush yards | Yards/carry, calculated | Rush TD | Targets | Receptions | Rec. yards | Rec. TD |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Ashton Jeanty | 8 | 15 | 1.88 | 0 | 8 | 8 | 61 | 1 |
| Mike Washington Jr. | 6 | 27 | 4.50 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

- Jeanty: **16 touches, 76 scrimmage yards**. Washington: six touches. Fullback Connor Heyward also caught one pass for 13 yards on a fake punt; do not silently count him as one of the two RBs.
- Jeanty/Washington combined: **14 carries, 42 yards, 3.00 yards/carry**. Team rushing: **17 carries, 48 yards, 2.82 yards/carry**, including Cousins' three carries for six yards. These are different denominators. The official recap's prose says 49 team rushing yards; we use 48, which reconciles to the individual totals and FootballDB's statistical table.
- Jeanty had **57.1% of the two RBs' carries** (8/14); Washington had 42.9%. That is a carry share, not a snap or route share.
- PFR reports **unofficial** offensive snaps of **49/68 (72%) for Jeanty and 18/68 (26%) for Washington**.[3] Retain PFR's snap denominator; FootballDB's 64 statistical offensive plays are not an interchangeable denominator. A primary gamebook participation table was not obtained.
- Both Washington and Puka Nacua participated, resolving our **Washington active / Puka active** branch. The [Rams' official game-day report][7] confirms Landman and McDuffie were inactive. No verified Washington workload restriction was found; participation does not establish full health.

**Same-source recent role comparison:** PFR's [Week 6 game page][4] reports Jeanty at 38/66 snaps (58%), with 10 carries and six targets; Washington at 23/66 (35%), with nine carries and one target. In Week 7 Jeanty's reported snap share increased and his two-RB carry share rose from 52.6% to 57.1%, even as his absolute carries fell from ten to eight. Washington remained meaningful competition, but a **new Week 7 demotion** is not supported by that comparison. These two games do not establish a permanent rotation.

**Weeks 1–6 outcome baseline:** FootballDB's [2026 game log][6] totals **98 carries for 313 yards (3.19/carry), 26 catches for 251 yards, and 36 targets across six games**. Week 7's eight carries were a season low and its 1.88/carry was below that already modest rushing baseline; eight catches were a season high. These are outcome comparisons, not opponent-adjusted talent estimates. FootballDB's 36 pregame targets differ from the frozen PFF 35 / SIS-attributed 37 in the intake. We have not reconciled those definitions; no mixed-provider target/route rate is calculated.



##### 2. Complete rushing ledger: poor results existed before the large deficit

The following transcribes **all eight official Jeanty carries** from [ESPN's play-by-play][5]. Clocks are ESPN's remaining game clock; scores are **before** the play, LAR–LV. This is a text-event ledger, not film classification.

| Quarter / clock | Score | Down/distance and location | Recorded direction | Gain |

| Q1 14:52 | 0–0 | 1st & 10, LV 38 | Middle | 3 |
| Q1 6:16 | 7–0 | 1st & 10, midfield | Middle | 8 |
| Q1 5:41 | 7–0 | 2nd & 2, LAR 42 | Middle | 1 |
| Q1 3:01 | 7–0 | 1st & 10, LAR 14 | Right guard | 2 |
| Q1 2:22 | 7–0 | 2nd & 8, LAR 12 | Left guard | -5 |

| Q3 8:30 | 28–6 | 1st & 10, LAR 30 | Middle | 0 |
| Q4 13:58 | 28–12 | 1st & 10, LAR 22 | Left guard | 5 |

The gains sum to **15**. Two of eight gained zero or negative yards (**25%**); six of eight gained three yards or fewer (**75%**). His longest was eight; none gained a rushing first down. These are transparent descriptions of gains, **not** contact-behind-the-line, stuffed-run, or down-adjusted success-rate measurements.

Five carries for nine yards occurred in Q1 with the score tied or within seven points. There were no Jeanty carries in Q2. Therefore the eventual deficit cannot explain away all of the poor rushing output. Conversely, Las Vegas trailed **28–6 at halftime and through Q3**, making reduced rushing volume and greater passing demand plausible. We cannot allocate the missing carries between game state, rotation and coaching decisions from totals alone.

Washington's complete rushing gain sequence was **21, -2, 2, 0, 4, 2**. His 21-yard run came in Q1 with Las Vegas down 7–0. The other five carries gained six yards; that sensitivity check does **not** replace his full 6-for-27 line or discount the explosive. His four Q3 carries included consecutive attempts from the LAR 15 and 13, including 3rd-and-2. Jeanty had two Q1 carries inside the 20; neither back had a carry inside the five. We can verify Washington received red-zone and short-yardage work; we cannot establish a goal-line takeover.

**What Washington's participation establishes:** real competition for a small rushing pool, including meaningful situations.  
**What it does not establish:** that his six attempts would all have gone to Jeanty if inactive, that he faced equivalent blocking/boxes/run designs, or that the 4.50-versus-1.88 average proves superior runner-created value. His explosive was recorded around the left end; most Jeanty attempts were recorded inside. Those are not controlled comparisons.



##### 3. Receiving utilization supported fantasy value; creation remains a separate question

Jeanty's eight official receiving gains were **-2, 4, 5, 22, 8, 4, 5, 15**, totaling 61.[5] The Q2 reception erased by accepted offensive holding is a separate **no-play** event, not a ninth official catch/target. Preserve it for any future film review without adding it to official statistics.

- By quarter: **Q1 3 catches/7 yards; Q2 2/30; Q3 2/9; Q4 1/15 and a TD**. Seven catches and 46 yards preceded Q4. Receiving involvement was not confined to the closing minutes.
- The TD was at **13:12 remaining in Q4 on ESPN's clock**, with the score 28–12 before the play; after the failed conversion it was 28–18. The [official team recap][8] describes a wheel route, and the [official clip page][9] describes an end-zone catch. That identifies a downfield receiving event; it does not establish elite route separation or tackle breaking. We should not characterize it as a last-minute consolation score.
- Under ordinary yardage/TD scoring, with no bonuses: **21.6 full PPR, 17.6 half PPR, 13.6 non-PPR**, calculated from the official line. Full PPR decomposes into **1.5 rushing + 6.1 receiving-yardage + 8 reception + 6 receiving-TD points**. Even removing the TD arithmetically leaves 15.6 PPR, although that is not a projection or a guaranteed floor.

The receiving-opportunity branch gains support: eight targets were realized alongside Washington's participation. **Elite receiving creation remains unresolved.** Catches, raw receiving yards and a wheel-route touchdown do not measure missed tackles, first-defender wins, YAC over expected or independent route separation.

**Usage-data limitation:** [NBC's postgame note][10] reports 20 routes on 35 team dropbacks. Its dropback denominator is unexplained alongside the verified 44 Cousins pass attempts, plus the fake-punt attempt. We do not adopt that as a full-game route total or calculate route participation/targets per route. Week 7 routes, true passing-down/two-minute snap shares, receiving-only missed tackles and a comparable YAC/creation assessment remain unverified. FootballDB's retrieved receiving tables omitted column headings, so we do not promote the trailing unlabeled values into a verified YAC measurement. We also do not roll the frozen pregame PFF counts forward without new evidence.

Until film or consistently scoped contact data are admitted, we assign none of these plays to a causal bucket. Linderbaum's postgame assessment that blocking needed improvement is relevant participant testimony,[8] not play-specific proof or an exoneration of runner decisions.



##### 6. What changes entering Week 8 at the Jets

**Our update:** reduce confidence in an imminent rushing-efficiency rebound; retain confidence that receiving involvement can keep Jeanty useful despite weak rushing. Washington remains a meaningful rushing competitor. The Jets trip is confirmed in the official recap,[8] but this note does not establish the strength of that matchup.

For PPR decisions, Jeanty remains a receiving-supported starting candidate, with roster alternatives and Week 8 health still relevant. In formats awarding fewer reception points, the weak rushing and uncertain scoring opportunities matter more. We should neither upgrade his rushing outlook because he scored 21.6 PPR nor infer a permanent talent failure from eight attempts.

The next evidence that would change our view is: **a sustained change in RB carry/snap concentration; verified improvement in rushing access and execution; continued targets supported by route participation; or a change in short-yardage/goal-line deployment.** Eight targets, eight catches and a TD are observations, not a Week 8 guarantee.

**Status:** The postgame outcome/role assessment is complete at this evidence level. Access attribution, advanced rushing/receiving creation and full film acceptance remain open; keep the issue open for those witnesses. The prospective hypotheses above remain unchanged.

## 6. Unknowns and revision triggers

##### 5. Remaining film and participation questions

A complete follow-up should chart **all eight rushes and eight official receptions**, with the nullified reception retained separately. Reviewer identity, definitions, source play references, uncertainty and disagreements must accompany any classifications.

1. **Rushing access:** first penetration/contact relative to the line, available lane at the decision point, blocking assignments/execution, fronts/boxes, run design and runner choice. Prioritize examining the Q1 -5 and the short-yardage failures, while retaining every attempt.
2. **Creation after access:** yards before/after contact, identifiable missed tackles, decisions and explosive opportunities. Do not derive contact location from gain or tackler identity. Compare with Weeks 1–6 only using equivalent definitions.
3. **Receiving creation:** catch location, immediate pressure/open space, blocking, first credible tackle opportunity and result; assess pre-catch separation separately. The negative reception, 22-yard catch and end-zone TD answer different process questions.
4. **Role causality:** complete personnel/route participation by drive and game state; pass protection versus routes, third downs, two-minute work, and any reported health restriction. Carry events alone cannot supply these snap-level classifications.

Official [game highlights and the linked Jeanty/Washington clips][11] are available as review leads. We retrieved page descriptions, not a full-film witness; highlight selection is insufficient to grade the complete attempt population. PFR and ESPN also differ on some event clocks; the ledger above consistently uses ESPN, and clock differences are not silently treated as separate plays.
