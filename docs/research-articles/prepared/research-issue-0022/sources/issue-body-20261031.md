## Why we chose Holani

Joe's starting question was about work that big-play highlights tend to omit: **Does Holani receive meaningful passing-down and pass-protection work alongside Jadarian Price, and what does he actually do on those snaps?**

The exploratory expectation was a complementary role: protecting the quarterback, contributing on some short-yardage plays, and potentially running enough routes to matter for Joe's personal fantasy decisions. Joe was also interested in Price's rushing ability and whether Holani could eventually support a flex-level workload.

These were operator hypotheses, not established usage facts. Joe had already encountered game reactions and some outcome information before watching. This was a retrospective learning session, not a blinded evaluation or a timestamped pregame preregistration. We must not reconstruct a stronger prior prediction after seeing the tape.

A stuffed carry can have several causes; a sack can occur despite a useful RB block; a route can be run without a target. The exercise was intended to preserve those distinctions and learn what the scoreboard and box score leave unexplained.

## How the session worked

- **Viewing:** Joe used his DAZN access. Game in 40 moved too quickly to inspect setup, so he switched to full replay, paused, rewound and inspected some plays repeatedly.
- **Learning level:** Familiar with Madden and football content, learning formal charting vocabulary. He asked Codex to pace corrections gently and become stricter as fluency improved.
- **Coverage:** Joe attempted to describe each play through both teams' possessions and all four quarters, ending with New England's final interception. He also described penalties, substitutions, stoppages and selected replays. A recovered missed play and multiple self-corrections were retained.
- **Important limit:** Reaching the end of the replay does not certify complete, accurate snap coverage. Player identities, clocks, formations and outcomes still contain unresolved values. Replay descriptions are not additional snaps.
- **Division of work:** Joe watched moving footage and supplied voice narration and selected phone photographs. Codex organized the transcript into local play entries, reviewed the supplied stills, explained terminology and made limited external source checks. Codex did not independently watch the complete moving game.
- **Personal scope:** These are learning notes for Joe's personal TIBER context. They are not a professional charting dataset or production evidence admission.

For every formation, Joe attempted this order:

1. **Personnel:** e.g. 11, 12, 21.
2. **QB set:** Under, Gun or Pistol.
3. **Backfield:** occupants, depth and alignment.
4. **Surface:** receiver/TE distribution, e.g. 2x2, 3x1, bunch or tight.
5. **Motion/shift:** movement after the initial alignment.

He then described the play action, player role, target/result, estimated gain and relevant uncertainty. Quarter, game clock, down/distance and field position were included when noticed. For future sessions, quarter/clock/down should precede the formation call to improve matching.

“Unreported,” “uncertain,” and explicitly observed “none” are different values. A blurry picture or incomplete identification remains unknown.

## Proposed next task for TIBER: one-drive ledger/PBP reconciliation pilot

**Question:** Can TIBER help an operator learn by matching narrated observations to real game events, preserving disagreement, and adding only corrections its evidence actually supports?

Start with **Seattle drive 2**, including Holani's carries/protection, Price's substitution and the stopped third-and-two run. This is a small, contiguous sequence with a useful still-image example.

### Step 1 — Establish actual source availability

Read the relevant existing ledger contract and the actual TIBER PBP source for this specific **2026-09-09 NE-at-SEA game**. Record the dataset/provider, source revision or digest, retrieval time, available fields, canonical game identity and relevant play IDs.

The gamebook is a comparison source, not a substitute for inspecting what TIBER actually ingested. If the game or connection is unavailable, report that precisely; do not substitute a different season/game, synthesize rows or imply the feed was checked.

### Step 2 — Prepare personal candidate ledger entries

Use the existing ledger contract where applicable rather than adopting the following as a new schema. A candidate needs to preserve:

- operator and local observation ID;
- game/quarter, approximate clock, clock type, down/distance, field position and neighboring-play sequence;
- the five formation fields in order;
- player identity, alignment and observed action phases;
- reported outcome, estimates and explicit unknowns;
- original account, self-correction history and evidence references;
- nullified/pre-snap/replay status;
- eventual PBP game/play ID, match status and supporting source.

Keep these entries in the operator's personal scope. References to existing camp/thesis entries must be verified before linking; Joe's recollection that an entry exists is not evidence that it was retrieved. The existing camp/signal ledger, personal chart observations and Research's attempt ledger must not be assumed to be the same store or contract.

### Step 3 — Match and reconcile without silent replacement

Use game identity, quarter, clock, possession, down/distance, field position and adjacent sequence together. A narrated clock may be after the play; do not demand an exact-clock join or force a unique match.

For each field, preserve:

**Original observation → source value → agreement/disagreement/unavailable → reviewed value, reason and evidence.**

Ambiguous and unmatched observations remain explicit. Neither the narrator nor PBP is automatically infallible. A binary shotgun flag alone may not settle Under versus Pistol, and ordinary PBP may not contain personnel, coverage, routes or protection responsibilities. Missing source fields do not count as operator errors.

### Step 4 — Return a bounded comparison

The deliverable should contain the candidate ledger mapping, one row per reconciled event with field-level differences, unresolved questions, and a short explanation Joe can use while learning.

Report match coverage separately from agreement among comparable fields. Do not calculate route/block/snap percentages until identities, no-play handling and the relevant denominators are reconciled. Chips followed by releases must not turn into two snaps; non-target routes must remain distinguishable from targets.
