# Carolina Week 1 → Week 2: reconstruction plan

## Why this case exists

[Research #25](https://github.com/Prometheus-Frameworks/TIBER-Research/issues/25), inspected October 1, records the official schedule's contrast: Carolina 37–59 Chicago in W1 and Carolina 34–3 Atlanta in W2. This packet treats these as **issue-reported historical results with the linked official source**, not independently recomputed fantasy DST outcomes. No new dataset or gamebook was acquired here.

The case was selected after the contrast was known. It is a diagnostic stress test of attribution and cutoff discipline, never a held-out success validating a selection rule. The useful question is whether a defensible pre-W2 process could have considered Carolina among the options actually eligible then, and what it could not have known.

## Two outputs, separate permissions and claims

### A. Retrospective diagnostic

Minimum closed scope: 2026 REG W1 `2026_01_CHI_CAR`, W1 `2026_01_ATL_PIT`, W2 `2026_02_CAR_ATL`; exactly both reciprocal team rows per game, six team-game rows total, after verifying source-native keys. These are requested identities, not an assertion all six rows have been inspected in this preparation. Stop on identity mismatch rather than alias coercion.

Recover exact retained W1 and W2 TTS reports and original source bindings/reviews. Verify complete input manifests, byte hashes, field names/units, game/team/opponent direction and duplicate/missing rows. Preserve provisional/finality/correction status. Use the reviewed ten-field reports where their purpose permits; do not run a producer to recreate missing artifacts.

Permitted later descriptive table, after Ops activation: passing attempts, completions, passing yards/TDs/interceptions, sacks suffered, carries, rushing yards/TDs, total fumbles lost. These are team activity observations. Optional arithmetic labels must spell out numerator/denominator and provenance; attempts plus sacks is only an explicitly labeled box-score proxy and not complete dropbacks. Zero-denominator ratios are unavailable.

Direction check: Carolina's sacks suffered describe Carolina's offense. Chicago's sacks suffered concern Chicago's offense in the game against Carolina, and can supply a reciprocal opponent-activity observation; credited Carolina defensive sacks require the source/attribution gate. Atlanta W1 offense supplies next-opponent context, not a Carolina defensive observation. Interceptions thrown and total fumbles lost cannot automatically be credited to a particular opposing DST unit or scoring category. Total fumbles can involve offense, defense or special teams, and event attribution is necessary.

Do not transform the score contrast into a fantasy DST score. Need verified scoring events (including sacks, interceptions, fumble recoveries, safeties, blocked kicks and return TDs), league scoring rules and points-allowed attribution. Field position, short fields, possessions, return scores and offensive turnovers can make the final team score differ from a simple measure of defensive quality.

### B. Verified pregame replay

Use the candidate study's Tuesday W2 cutoff, with an exact UTC instant frozen only after the qualified schedule/date is bound. A separately labeled Thursday or Sunday cutoff can be another decision unit. Do not substitute a guessed date or current-week clock. Need every feature version's demonstrable pre-cutoff availability, the eligible alternatives at that same cutoff, supported baselines and scoring profile. Retained reports generated/acquired after the game can support retrospective description but not historical knowledge without a separate exact-version availability witness.

W2 rows are outcomes only. W1 rows may be predictive inputs only if their exact revisions and availability satisfy the W2 cutoff. W1→W2 comparison output itself includes W2 outcomes and must never enter a pre-W2 feature set. Compare all eligible alternatives under the same rule and cutoff; a Carolina-only replay cannot establish selection superiority.

## Competing explanations

| Hypothesis | Needed supporting evidence | Counterevidence / competing evidence | Current disposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| W1 final points concealed a useful Carolina process signal | Supported defensive sack events/exposure, scoring-event attribution and field position; available before cutoff | Low pressure/exposure or opponent errors driving sacks; mostly favorable-score hindsight | Unresolved; box activity alone is incomplete |
| W2 opponent/context was materially more favorable | Atlanta W1 offensive exposure/vulnerability, pregame QB/OL status, dated expectations and venue/weather | Personnel/news unavailable by cutoff; hindsight final Atlanta score substituted | Unresolved; next-opponent framing is available, causal explanation is not |
| W2 was mostly outcome variance without a selectable edge | Decomposed turnovers, return TDs, possession/scoring events and comparison with pregame baselines | A reproducible pre-cutoff mechanism that generalizes across successes and failures | Unresolved; no counterfactual/edge established |

Support may coexist across explanations. One case cannot estimate stable effects or identify causality. A final report may legitimately say “descriptively different, predictive explanation unverified.”

## Exact missing witnesses

1. **Custody/purpose:** byte-authenticated W1/W2 report packages, original reviews, complete manifests and scope that permits this diagnostic reuse. Public comments reporting an artifact are locators, not byte inspection.
2. **Historical availability:** exact-version availability receipts for each pregame feature and comparison baseline. Retrieval, report generation, review and commit times do not prove earlier publication.
3. **Scoring attribution:** licensed/eligible event-level DST and kicking outcomes plus explicit scoring profile. Final team scores do not suffice.
4. **Denominators/environment:** defensive snaps/dropbacks/possessions, pressure definitions, start field position, score/time context and offense-versus-defense-versus-ST points/event classification.
5. **Personnel/context:** source-native QB/kicker IDs, pre-cutoff injury/job/OL reports and roof/weather forecasts; later status reports cannot stand in.
6. **Availability:** dated league waiver/free-agent/lock snapshot and permitted use; otherwise an explicitly simulated pool, with no claim Carolina was available in Joe's league.
7. **Markets/experts:** contemporaneous versions, permitted retention/use, timestamps and provider identity. No qualified market or expert baseline was established by this preparation.

These are missing **for this packet's readiness claim**. The inventory distinguishes fields absent from the inspected ten-field TTS binding from potentially existing external or retained records not verified here. Do not label everything unavailable globally.

## Falsification and generalization

Before later evaluation, freeze the same DST rules for all eligible teams/weeks; include bad defenses that stayed bad and strong prior results that regressed. Preserve rare scoring events in the real fantasy target. The outcome-selected Carolina case can expose data/attribution problems and inspire a hypothesis, but any resulting hypothesis needs a new, uncontaminated evaluation window. Kicker mechanisms and targets remain a separate study.
