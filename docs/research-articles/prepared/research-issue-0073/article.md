### Five questions that shape the game

#### 1 Can Pittsburgh keep Cleveland's rush off Rodgers

**Q1a evidence card — Pittsburgh protection against Cleveland's front; links Q1b.** The working explanation is that Cleveland's rush can again stop drives, as it did in Week 4. The credible alternative is that Pittsburgh's quick release and stronger run game keep Rodgers in manageable downs, so the pass rush gets fewer chances.

**Evidence:** Rodgers was sacked **5 times in Week 4 at Cleveland** and **22 times in seven games**, calculated as 22 of 285 attempts-plus-sacks, **7.7%**. Four of his seven games had four or more sacks. The [Yahoo Week 4 recap](https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/thursday-night-football-deshaun-watson-035438233.html) records the five Week 4 sacks. Sharp attributes a season-high **42.2% pressure rate** on Rodgers to Cleveland in that game. The [Steelers' "Matt's Stats"](https://www.steelers.com/news/matt-s-stats-week-8-vs-browns) (October 29) reports a Cleveland **7% sack rate (4th)**, a league-high **46 tackles for loss**, and Pittsburgh's **2.71-second** time to throw, which only three offenses beat. It also reports **14 Rodgers sacks on third down**.

**Interpretation:** a fast-release offense still taking many sacks points to more than just the line: long passing downs, coverage holding up, and Rodgers holding the ball on some plays. These figures can't separate those causes. Jared Verse and Mason Graham practiced fully on Friday and have no game status, so Cleveland's front looks largely intact. Graham was marked injured on Tuesday's depth chart; whether he plays a full workload is unknown.

**Sample and metric fit:** official sack counts and an attributed pressure rate whose provider and definition aren't exposed. Sacks undercount total pressure. Time to throw excludes sacks under the Next Gen Stats definition, so a 2.71 average doesn't describe the failed dropbacks. No individual rusher-versus-blocker alignment is claimed.

**Confidence and revision:** high in the observed sack totals. Moderate that protection is the main constraint on Pittsburgh's passing game. Low on how big the effect will be. Early-down rushing success that keeps Pittsburgh out of third-and-long would weaken the case. Repeated free rushers or sacks on early downs would strengthen it. **Watch:** Pittsburgh's distance on third down and whether sacks come on early or late downs (visible on the broadcast). **Status: supported baseline, mixed projection.**

**Q1b evidence card — Pittsburgh receivers against Cleveland's man coverage; links Q1a.** Sharp reports Cleveland in man coverage on **71.1%** of Week 4 snaps, and Rodgers facing man on a league-high **40.4%** of dropbacks, at **6.0 Y/A (22nd)** and **51.8% completions (27th)** against it. The working explanation is that Pittsburgh's receivers struggle to beat man coverage regularly. The alternative is that Metcalf's size and Week 4 production (**5 catches, 115 yards**) are enough to punish single coverage.

Matt's Stats says Metcalf has **four or fewer receptions in five of seven games**. It also gives Germie Bernard a **27% target share over three starts** and Pat Freiermuth **1.08 yards per route run, 34th of 42 tight ends**. Skowronek is questionable with a calf injury after missing two games. Roman Wilson had 3 catches for 74 yards in Week 4 but isn't on the current depth chart; his role now wasn't established. Sharp says passes to Pittsburgh's wide receivers succeed **39.9%** of the time against a 48.8% league average, using its own success definition.

**Sample and limit:** one same-opponent game plus attributed season rates without exposed denominators. Coverage-type splits come from one provider and can't be checked here. Target share is not route share. **Confidence:** moderate that Pittsburgh's receivers have had trouble separating; low on transfer to this game. **Revision trigger:** Metcalf or Bernard repeatedly winning in single coverage early in the game, or Cleveland changing its coverage mix. **Watch:** whether Rodgers' early completions go to backs and tight ends or to wideouts down the field. **Status: mixed.**

#### 2 Can Cleveland's right side hold up against Watt

**Q2a evidence card — Howard's status and the right-tackle matchup; links Q2b.** Howard left Week 7 with a head injury in the first half and didn't return ([NBC, Oct. 25](https://www.nbcsports.com/fantasy/football/player-news/2026-10-25/tytus-howard-exits-with-concussion)). He was DNP Wednesday through Friday and is **questionable**. Cleveland's depth chart lists Dawand Jones behind him. Pittsburgh's depth chart lists Watt at left outside linebacker. That usually lines him up across from the offense's right tackle, but the label is not a measured alignment. Watt has **5.5 sacks**, Highsmith 3 and Herbig 2 per the Steelers stats page. Highsmith was limited Wednesday and Thursday with a groin injury and full on Friday.

**Interpretation:** three missed practices with a concussion make Jones starting a realistic scenario, but **questionable is not ruled out**, and the inactive list will settle it. Neither tackle should be assumed to be beaten. Watt and Highsmith move around, and Cleveland can help with chips and tight ends.

**Sample and fit:** official injury statuses, a depth-chart label and season sack counts. No pass-block grades or rush-side charting were checked. **Confidence:** high in the status facts, low on the effect of the matchup. **Revision trigger:** Howard active; Pittsburgh lining Watt up on the left side regularly; Cleveland giving help consistently on that side. **Watch:** who starts at RT and how often a tight end or back stays in to help on that side (broadcast suggests it; responsibility needs film). **Status: unresolved availability.**

**Q2b evidence card — Watson's short passing game against a thinned Pittsburgh secondary; links Q2a and Q3.** The working explanation is that Cleveland moves the ball with short throws and yards after the catch, which also limits sacks. The alternative is that Watson holds the ball, as in Weeks 5–6, and Pittsburgh's rush turns drives into sacks.

**Evidence:** Watson was sacked **13 times in Weeks 5–6** (8 at the Jets, 5 vs Baltimore) but only **once in Week 7** and **twice in Week 4 against Pittsburgh**, when he went 24/33 for 268 yards. His season sack rate is calculated as 25 of 235 attempts-plus-sacks, **10.6%**. Matt's Stats gives **8%** and calls it league-worst, probably with a different denominator. Matt's Stats also reports a **3.07-second** Cleveland time to throw (seventh highest), **56.4%** of Watson's passing yards after the catch (most in the league), and **29.2%** completions on deep attempts. Sharp gives **43.1%** (25 of 58) on throws of 10+ yards. These are different depth thresholds and are kept separate.

Pittsburgh is without CB Brandin Echols and DB Doneiko Slaughter. Ramsey (wrist) practiced fully Thursday and Friday and has no game status. Matt's Stats credits Pittsburgh with a league-high **45 pass breakups**. Cleveland CB Myles Harden and S Daniel Thomas are questionable, which matters for Pittsburgh's passing game, not Cleveland's.

**Interpretation:** Week 4 showed Cleveland can run its quick game against this defense, and the Yahoo recap credits Pittsburgh's second-half adjustment with stopping it. The missing depth defenders matter most in nickel and dime packages. This preview doesn't claim a specific coverage matchup.

**Metric fit:** official sacks and attempts. Attributed time to throw, YAC share and depth splits with differing thresholds. Sack rate depends on the denominator, so both are reported. **Confidence:** high in the sack history; moderate that the short game is Cleveland's most dependable passing path; low on avoiding sacks this week. **Revision trigger:** early sacks on non-obvious passing downs. **Watch:** how quickly Watson gets the ball out on early downs, and whether the YAC plays keep working after halftime. **Status: mixed.**

#### 3 Was Concepcion's Week 7 a new role or one big day

**Q3 evidence card — links Q2b; continues [TIBER study #60](https://github.com/Prometheus-Frameworks/TIBER-Research/issues/60).** The working explanation is a steady share of targets that pays off more in a quick-game plan. The alternative is that Week 7's **8 catches, 129 yards and 3 rushes for 23** came mostly from efficiency within an existing role.

**Evidence:** #60 records **35 targets in Weeks 1–6** (5.8 per game, with Week 5 shortened by a first-quarter concussion), then **10 targets in Week 7**. Against Pittsburgh in Week 4 he had **5 catches for 64 yards on 8 targets**. Matt's Stats reports his **targets per route run at 35.7%** in Week 7, against a previous high of 25%; **55 yards after contact**, the most by a receiver in a game this season; and a **47.8% slot rate**, with no window given. The [official Week 7 box](https://www.clevelandbrowns.com/game-day/2026/reg-week7/browns-at-titans/box-score) also shows Fannin with 6 catches for 91 yards, and Boston and Jeudy involved, so the breakout didn't depend on teammates being absent.

**Interpretation:** a 35.7% targets-per-route rate is one game's rate and will very likely come down. Week 4 shows he already earned real volume against this defense. The useful question is whether the short-game plan in Q2b keeps sending designed touches to him, not whether 129 yards repeats.

**Sample and limit:** retained #60 counts from ESPN rows checked against the official log; one-game attributed route metrics from an unnamed provider. **Confidence:** high in the counts; low in role persistence. **Revision trigger:** sustained targets with comparable route participation, carries by design, and slot usage. **Watch:** early designed touches (screens, jet motion, carries) and whether he plays in two-receiver sets. **Status: unresolved.**

#### 4 Can Pittsburgh's backs carry the offense against Cleveland's run defense

**Q4 evidence card — links Q1a; continues [#61](https://github.com/Prometheus-Frameworks/TIBER-Research/issues/61) and [#66](https://github.com/Prometheus-Frameworks/TIBER-Research/issues/66).** The working explanation is that Warren and Dowdle can stay efficient and keep Pittsburgh out of the long passing downs where Q1a's risk is concentrated. The alternative is that Cleveland's tackle-for-loss front creates negative runs and pushes Pittsburgh back to the pass.

**Evidence:** Warren is at **5.50 yards per carry** (102–561). He had **17 carries for 93 yards in Week 4** and **18 for 101 in Week 7**. Matt's Stats reports Pittsburgh at **4.64 yards per rush (8th)**, **3.14 yards after contact (4th)** and a **6% explosive-run rate (6th)**, and Warren at **6.8 yards per touch**, best among players with 75 or more touches. Cleveland allows a calculated **4.43 per rush**, and Sharp puts Cleveland's run-defense success rate allowed at **58.7% (23rd)**. On the other side, Cleveland leads the league with **46 tackles for loss**, and Sharp ranks its pass defense 3rd by success rate. Pittsburgh still passes at the second-highest rate in the league, and **92.9%** of its offensive touchdowns have come through the air (Matt's Stats). Sharp reports **9 TDs on 19 red-zone trips (27th)** with a league-high **68.6% pass rate in the red zone**.

#66 found Dowdle with **15 opportunities in Week 7** (9 carries, 6 targets), with three carries and one catch on the final clock-killing drive. Neither back is on the Friday injury report. Dowdle had a knee listing before Week 7.

**Interpretation:** Pittsburgh's run game is efficient but not used much. The question is whether McCarthy leans on it more to protect Rodgers. Cleveland's tackle-for-loss total suggests the risk is getting stopped behind the line, not a soft run defense, so a decent per-carry average can still hide drive-killing losses.

**Metric fit:** official carries and yards; attributed success rates and per-contact splits from different providers. They aren't combined. **Confidence:** high in Warren's efficiency so far; moderate that the run game is Pittsburgh's best counter to the rush; low on how much play-calling changes. **Revision trigger:** repeated early-down runs for no gain or a loss; Pittsburgh passing on most first downs even with a lead. **Watch:** Pittsburgh's run/pass mix on first down and how its backs split the work. **Status: mixed.**

#### 5 Does Cleveland's run game exist against this front

**Q5 evidence card — medium priority; links Q2b.** The working explanation is that Pittsburgh's run defense keeps Judkins inefficient and puts more of the load on Watson. The alternative is that Week 7's **17 carries, 96 yards and 2 TD** points to a better run game, with Watson's legs adding to it.

**Evidence:** Judkins' first five games total **73 carries for 221 yards, 3.03 per carry**, with **17 for 53 (3.1)** against Pittsburgh in Week 4. He has no Week 6 line on the NFL log, and the reason wasn't checked. Week 7 came against Tennessee. Pittsburgh allows a calculated **3.75 per rush**, and Matt's Stats notes Pittsburgh held New Orleans to 18 rushing yards on 17 carries in Week 7 ([Steelers press-room account](https://www.steelers.com/news/from-the-press-room-steelers-vs-saints-x3526)). Watson has at least **19 rushing yards in every game** (48–215 overall); the NFL log doesn't separate scrambles from designed runs. Dylan Sampson (knee, full Thursday and Friday) had 6 carries for 45 yards in Week 7.

**Interpretation:** Week 7 is one game against a different opponent. Cleveland's offense more likely works through quick passing and Watson's legs than through Judkins volume. **Confidence:** high in the observed splits; low that Week 7's efficiency repeats. **Revision trigger:** Cleveland staying on schedule with first-down runs. **Watch:** Judkins' first-down carries and whether Sampson takes passing-down work. **Status: supported baseline, unresolved projection.**

## 5. Interpretation and assessment

### Game scenarios and fantasy implications

**Leading scenario:** a low-scoring, pressure-heavy game. Both offenses take sacks. Pittsburgh's better run game and run defense give it a slight edge at home, while Cleveland stays in the game through Watson's quick throws, Concepcion and Fannin after the catch, and its own pass rush. Short fields and turnovers may decide it more than sustained drives.

**Credible alternative:** Cleveland repeats Week 4. Its man coverage holds up against Pittsburgh's receivers, the rush hurts Rodgers on third down, and Watson avoids the Weeks 5–6 sacks. This becomes more likely if Howard plays, Pittsburgh's run game stalls early, or Skowronek is out and Pittsburgh's receiver group gets thinner.

These are analyst scenarios, not calibrated forecasts. A Pittsburgh run-heavy script and a Rodgers high-volume script are different paths; the player outcomes in each don't stack.
