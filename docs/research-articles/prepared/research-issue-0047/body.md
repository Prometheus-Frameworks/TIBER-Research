## 2. Question and scope

### Preserved study context (navigation, not a new evidence cut)

2025 history and 2026 Weeks1–6, including separate Weeks3–4 concentration and post-Harrison Weeks5–6. Intended Z role is not Puka-equivalent performance; McBride-led distribution remains an alternative. The original and this edition retain their own cutoffs; no later result is imported backward.

#### Question origin and initial assessment

The operator identified Wilson's late-2025 breakout, the offseason Puka Nacua comparison, consecutive high-volume games in 2026 Weeks 3–4, seven targets in each game since Harrison's departure, and only one receiving touchdown through six games. The operator asked whether the role comparison has substance; do not turn that question into an asserted belief that Wilson will reproduce Nacua's results.

**Starting assessment:** the comparison has an attributable scheme/role basis, and Wilson has demonstrated very high target concentration in two games. A sustained version of that concentration is not established. His recent target share fell despite Harrison's exit. Low touchdown production warrants an opportunity investigation, not an assumption that touchdowns are owed.



#### Prospective hypotheses

These explanations can coexist. Assess them against counterevidence; do not grade agreement with the operator or use a touchdown as a substitute for role evidence.

| Hypothesis | Evidence that strengthens it | Evidence that weakens it |
|---|---|---|
| **H1 — Sustained featured receiving role.** The Z deployment supports recurring high target concentration, beyond two spike games. | Target share rises and remains elevated across games; verified route access and targets per route support it; opportunities recur in ordinary possessions and alongside McBride. | Share stays near the recent lower window despite sustained route access, or route access itself contracts. A one-game spike remains insufficient. |
| **H2 — Stable leading-WR role with a constrained ceiling.** Seven-ish targets and variable production are typical within a McBride-led distribution. | Similar workload persists; McBride/Bourne/TE work remains substantial; strong scores require unusually efficient conversion or touchdowns. | Wilson repeatedly captures a larger share with broader valuable opportunities, including productive games without touchdowns. |
| **H3 — Defensive attention and/or passing execution suppress conversion.** The role exists, but target quality or execution limits results. | Attributable charting or inspected plays show difficult/unavailable opportunities, pressure, coverage effects or missed throws; Wilson maintains role while those conditions change. | Representative evidence shows catchable opportunities repeatedly lost through Wilson's execution, or reduced allocation better explains the decline. Separate these mechanisms rather than assigning blame from the box score. |
| **H4 — Scoring opportunity changes explain the touchdown gap.** A future scoring increase requires opportunities, not calendar repetition. | Red-zone/end-zone targets, team scoring possessions and credible designed opportunities expand, even before touchdowns arrive. | Those opportunities stay scarce, passing TDs continue going elsewhere, or only a single low-probability score changes the fantasy total. |



## 3. Baseline and alternatives

#### 2025 baseline — volume arrived before the touchdown surge

The inspected ESPN 2025 game log supports the operator's historical outline. [S1]

| Window | Targets | Receptions | Receiving yards | Receiving TD |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Weeks 1–8, including Week 8 bye | 27 | 15 | 136 | 1 |
| Weeks 9–18, ten games | 99 | 63 | 870 | 6 |
| Full regular season | 126 | 78 | 1,006 | 7 |

Window totals are calculations from the weekly log. Wilson's early touchdown was in Week 2. His next scores were two in Week 14, followed by one in each of Weeks 15–18. Thus he scored in all five final games, but had already recorded 18 targets/185 yards in Week 11 and 15 targets/118 yards in Week 12 without a touchdown.

**Implication:** a touchdown drought can coexist with substantial receiving opportunity. This sequence does not establish a recurring seasonal pattern, an expected 2026 touchdown total, or that the same opportunities exist under a new offense.



#### What the Puka comparison actually establishes

Arizona's August 26 account attributes the comparison to LaFleur. Wilson discussed playing the Z role occupied by Nacua and previously Cooper Kupp in the Rams offense, including the run-game responsibilities that accompany it. LaFleur's Rams background therefore gives this a concrete positional and schematic basis. [S3]

That is evidence of intended deployment, not equivalent ability, route assignments on every play, target entitlement or equal fantasy output. Test the analogy through actual alignment, motion/release access, route participation, blocking assignments, passing concepts and opportunity across game situations. None of those detailed deployment rates has been independently charted for this case.

- Mean targets are 9.7 per game; the median and four of six individual games are seven. The average alone conceals the two-game concentration.
- Weeks 3–4 were not merely identical target shares inflated by passing volume: Wilson accounted for 34.0% and 37.1% of credited targets. Whether that concentration recurs remains open.
- The two seven-target post-Harrison games represent different shares: 15.2% and 20.6%. Week 5 had 48 Arizona pass attempts, including Wilson's own trick-play attempt; Week 6 had 35. Credited targets differ from pass attempts. [B5–B6, S8]
- Wilson leads Arizona WRs in targets through this sample, but McBride has 65 to Wilson's 58. Being the team's leading WR does not establish first place among all receiving options. [B1–B6]
- Wilson has 5.52 receiving yards per target and a 56.9% catch rate. These are outcomes, not explanations: catchability, depth, pressure, coverage and execution have not been separated. Do not infer average target depth solely from yards per catch.
- Brissett is the passer in all six inspected game boxes. A mid-sample starting-QB change is not an established explanation for this particular split. Verify later changes rather than assuming continuity forever.

Fantasy rank is not used as a baseline claim because scoring format, platform and total-points versus per-game basis were not established. This ledger is receiving-only; it is not a complete fantasy-points ledger.



#### Harrison's departure and the actual surrounding offense

**Timeline:** the move was reported October 11; the NFL account says Arizona announced it Monday. Arizona's October 12 snap report explicitly identifies the Detroit game as the first game after Harrison. Weeks 5 and 6 can therefore be treated as the first two games without him, rather than classifying games solely from the announcement date. [S4–S5, S8]

Three observations challenge a simple automatic-volume-transfer thesis:

1. **Harrison had only 13 targets across Weeks 1–4** in the inspected boxes. Removing him did not vacate an established high-volume receiving workload. [B1–B4]
2. **Brooks inherited substantial receiver participation.** Arizona reported 39 snaps against Detroit and 38 against the Rams, approximately 53% in each game. The Week 5 account places him in Harrison's former assignment. [S5–S6, S8]
3. **Multiple tight ends remain central.** Arizona reported 25 snaps of 13 personnel against Detroit. McBride played 70 of 72 offensive snaps in Week 6; Higgins and Quitoriano also retained substantial work. These are snap/personnel observations, not interchangeable route counts. [S5–S6]

There is also evidence consistent with defensive attention: Arizona's October 11 account describes Detroit challenging the offense to win through receivers other than McBride and Wilson. Treat that as an attributed account of one game, not independently charted coverage rates or proof that coverage caused every unsuccessful target. [S8]

Wilson's own participation had already been substantial: the official Week 4 account reports 94% offensive snaps, the second time in three games he reached that level. More 13 personnel coincided with lower Harrison participation even before the trade. [S7]

**Interpretation:** the departure can change assignments and defensive choices without automatically adding targets to Wilson. McBride, Bourne, Brooks, the other TEs, passing volume and player availability all belong in the comparison.



##### Touchdown test

Wilson's one touchdown on 58 targets is descriptive. No expected-TD model, complete red-zone/end-zone target ledger or catchable-target ledger has been inspected. It is not enough to call him due for positive regression.

Track Wilson's red-zone and end-zone targets separately, plus Arizona's scoring trips/pass attempts, McBride/other TE usage near the goal line and actual pass design where visible. A Wilson touchdown with unchanged opportunity can improve that week's result while leaving the central thesis unresolved.
