#### Longer-horizon community model

The ambition is larger than replacing one dead injury feed.

A future TIBER evidence network could allow knowledgeable community contributors to manually chart observations that broad public datasets do not capture well: protection assignments, chips, route-versus-block participation, personnel deployment, coverage looks, first reads, role changes, or other tape-verifiable football events.

The desired pattern is not crowdsourced opinion. It is **crowdsourced observation with governed admission**.

A possible flow:

```text
human watches film / observes a public event
        ↓
structured claim or charting submission
  - exact game/play/player/team identity
  - observation class
  - source/film reference where permissible
  - timestamp and scope
  - contributor identity/reputation state
        ↓
independent human and/or agent verification
  - duplicate observation comparison
  - identity checks
  - contradiction detection
  - evidence sufficiency
  - observed vs inferred separation
        ↓
governed TIBER evidence record
  - provenance
  - confidence / agreement state
  - revision history
  - unresolved disagreement retained
        ↓
research and models consume only within declared authority
```

Agents are useful here primarily as assemblers, auditors, and consistency witnesses — not as a substitute for the original observation. For tape-derived claims, an agent could collect multiple independently charted observations, check player/play identity, compare definitions, surface disagreement, verify that the claimed evidence actually supports the stated observation, and produce a reliable report of what the witnesses collectively saw. Where verification cannot establish the claim, the system should retain disagreement or uncertainty rather than manufacture consensus.

#### Why an upstream contribution matters

If Lane 2 eventually produces something useful enough for a major open-football community project such as nflverse to consume, TIBER should approach that opportunity with reciprocal ambition: solve our own missing-witness problem in a form that may also become a public good.

That does **not** mean forcing TIBER-specific governance onto an upstream project. The upstream contribution should be simple, useful, maintainable, and compatible with that community's norms. TIBER can retain the richer provenance/admission layer internally.

The strategic value is two-sided:

- TIBER gains better witnesses and learns how to build against community-grade data contracts;
- the broader ecosystem may gain maintained open infrastructure;
- contributors and maintainers become potential collaborators rather than merely external data sources;
- TIBER demonstrates its philosophy through useful work rather than asking a community to trust a design document.

This is therefore also a relationship-building opportunity. Any future outreach should lead with working evidence, reproducibility, maintenance willingness, humility about the host project's norms, and a concrete contribution that is useful even to people who never use TIBER.
