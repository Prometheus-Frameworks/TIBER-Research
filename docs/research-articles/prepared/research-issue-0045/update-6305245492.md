His blocking/adjustment consistency has not been evaluated here. “Coen produces slot stars” is too coarse to identify Hunter's eventual job.

## 5. Interpretation and assessment

#### 6. Dynasty and redraft consequences

- **Dynasty:** receiving capability has more historical support than the tiny current offensive sample alone suggests. The longer-term question includes how Jacksonville chooses between his two valuable roles, plus his development and sustained workload. A receiving ceiling is not a forecast of which role the team will choose.
- **Redraft:** keep the readiness thesis separate from the activation trigger. Specific expanded offensive preparation, increased ordinary route/series participation, and defensive backfill would strengthen an actionable opportunity case. A teammate absence or one touchdown alone does not establish that transfer.
- **Week 8 test:** H1 strengthens with broader participation across possessions/concepts; H2 with continued selective packages; H3 with allocation changes that track documented availability/workload conditions. Preserve first-read status as unknown where the evidence cannot establish it.
- **BTJ:** this pass does not establish B1 system mismatch or diagnose his decline. The role-comparison framework is ready for a separate BTJ execution/deployment assessment; none of the Hunter examples transfers automatically to him.
