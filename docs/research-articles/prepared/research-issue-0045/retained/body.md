# Jacksonville receiving roles — Hunter, BTJ and Coen

**Opened October 20, 2026. First checkpoint: 2026 regular-season Week 8.**  
**Central question:** Does Travis Hunter become part of Jacksonville's regular passing structure, including plays where he is not the intended first read?

This is an operator-requested, living football research note. Its purpose is to explain how Liam Coen's offense distributes receiving work and how two talented receivers fit that structure. Touchdowns and fantasy points are outcomes to explain alongside role evidence. This issue hosts the initial assessment, prospective questions and dated follow-ups; it is not a machine-validated Research custody run or a promoted predictive finding.

## Starting assessment

Hunter has supplied a reason to investigate an expanded receiving role, but his tiny offensive sample cannot establish that expansion or its sustainability. BTJ's reduced participation is documented; “good player in the wrong system” remains one candidate explanation. Execution, quarterback connection and competition have to receive the same scrutiny.

The useful joint question is: **what earns a receiver a place in this passing structure, and what keeps him there when the ball goes elsewhere?** Hunter's and BTJ's opportunities are not automatically interchangeable.

## Hunter: competing explanations

| Candidate explanation | Evidence that would strengthen it | Evidence that would weaken it |
|---|---|---|
| **H1 — Broader receiving integration.** Hunter becomes a regular participant in passing concepts. | More routes relative to a consistently defined team passing denominator; participation across possessions, down/distance and alignments; meaningful routes on untargeted plays; several concept families; credible evidence of involvement beyond manufactured touches. | Brief appearances remain tightly clustered around selected touches, even if one produces a touchdown. |
| **H2 — Valuable offensive specialist.** He remains a near full-time defender with a limited offensive package. | Heavy defensive work persists; offensive usage remains selective and situation-specific; high target rate on few routes continues. | Broad offensive participation expands while defensive demands remain comparable. |
| **H3 — Usage constrained by availability and workload.** The allocation responds to the cornerback rotation, teammates' health or game conditions. | Offensive involvement changes alongside documented defensive rotation/health changes or specific receiver absences. | A broader receiving role persists across materially different availability and workload conditions. |

H1–H3 can coexist. Brown's return could create flexibility, but that is a proposed mechanism, not a promised offensive increase. Efficiency on a small, selective offensive sample cannot be assumed to persist unchanged across a broader receiving workload.

## BTJ: diagnose the break before prescribing an environment

Start with a sequence: **available to play → selected for a personnel grouping → assigned a route → executes the assignment → reaches a usable window in time → quarterback chooses and delivers → catch/result.** Missing evidence at one step is unknown, not proof the previous step failed.

Compare four explanations:

- **B1 — Deployment/fit:** the actual assignments or release conditions underuse his demonstrated strengths. Compare route family, alignment, motion, coverage, leverage and the timing of the concept with supported earlier examples.
- **B2 — Execution and selection:** catch execution, route detail, adjustments or blocking requirements reduce opportunities. Separate visible errors from vague statements about effort or attitude. “Trust” is a reported coaching assessment or an inference to test, never a diagnosed personality trait.
- **B3 — Quarterback connection:** timing, reads, placement or pressure prevent usable routes from becoming targets/completions. An untargeted route is not automatically a missed read.
- **B4 — Competition and personnel:** Washington, Meyers, tight ends or Cameron earn the available work; BTJ's declining volume can coexist with an otherwise functioning offense.

**Initial environment hypothesis, explicitly provisional:** evaluate an outside/intermediate/deep role with release help where useful, combinations that create clear leverage, and a quarterback able and willing to deliver on time. Also test whether accessible short/intermediate touches improve involvement. Describe required route, release and quarterback conditions before naming any team. A destination list or trade-price exercise is outside this study.

Importantly, Coen's own offseason emphasis already included BTJ downfield. If the proposed “better environment” simply repeats that intention, we still need to show what is missing in actual deployment or execution. Recovery within Jacksonville can support the mechanism just as a later team change might. A confirmed trade would introduce several simultaneous changes and would not isolate a coaching effect.
