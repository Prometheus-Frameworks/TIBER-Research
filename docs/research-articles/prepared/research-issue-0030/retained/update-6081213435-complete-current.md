### Proposed case specification for the existing readiness packet

1. **Baseline versus situation:** retain broad defensive measures, then separately show red-zone, third-down and fourth-down populations, with numerator/denominator and per-game coverage. Do not manufacture a combined strength score.
2. **Possession context:** distinguish sustained drives from short fields after turnovers; preserve starting field position, score/time, penalty extensions and credited/nullified events. Avoid double-counting linked failures as independent evidence.
3. **Functional attribution:** distinguish run fit/containment, rush/pressure and coverage only when qualified witnesses support them. A sack total cannot establish coverage quality; a completion cannot establish which defender failed. LB/safety rush and coverage functions cross position groups.
4. **Offense companion:** retain third-down distance, protection/pressure, QB identity and turnover-play context. Offensive line/QB/skill lenses remain companion questions, not an expansion of the first defensive experiment.
5. **Historical comparison:** use matching W1–W4 populations and compatible metric definitions. Record changed personnel, QB participation, opponents and game state; withhold "same offense" and "regression from last year" until supported.
6. **Future watch:** before inspecting future outcomes, freeze any later Q2 support/challenge criteria separately. This post-Q1 case does not backdate those criteria.

### What would make this case useful?

- A useful descriptive result may establish strong broad suppression plus poor situational outcomes, while leaving the responsible functional unit unresolved.
- If failures concentrate in short fields, penalty extensions or particular distances, narrow the original claim accordingly.
- If comparable legal-play failures recur across opponents/personnel, retain a situational weakness hypothesis for further testing.
- If qualified rush/coverage witnesses are missing, explicitly abstain from unit attribution.
- Unsupported rankings, no-denominator claims, post-cutoff inputs presented as pregame evidence, nullified sacks counted as credited sacks, and positional-to-causal shortcuts should fail the case's evidence checks.
- This selected case cannot demonstrate calibration, persistence, predictive improvement, or fantasy startability.

Lower defensive EPA allowed is better. These are **provider-reported descriptive splits**, not independently reproduced TIBER results. This supports the friend's current 31st-place claim at the inspected page; it does not authenticate last year's first-place claim. The original handoff's "unverified exact EPA rankings" status is therefore refined for this specific current provider report only.

The page labels season-tagged measures as season-to-date and notes that those cannot be recut by its week-range filter. Preserve the exact metric definition, qualifying play population, EPA model/source ancestry, penalty/no-play treatment and historical cutoff before formal reuse. The reported 21-play population is not reconciled to another provider's play count. Do not combine provider populations or average incompatible metrics. The page's quarter splits refer to quarters within games, not this investigation's W1–W4 season quarter.

**Interpretation to test:**
- Strong results outside the red zone and poor results inside it are compatible: the subsets concern different situations and have very different sample sizes.
- Twenty-one qualifying plays is a small, possession-clustered population. A few consequential outcomes can move the rank materially. This warrants investigation, not a persistent bottom-two ability label or an unsupported confidence interval.
- EPA identifies outcome deterioration within the provider's model. It cannot assign causal responsibility to the secondary, front, scheme or individual defenders.
- Existing W4 evidence of four 15-yard penalties on the Chargers' two touchdown drives makes penalty/possession extension a concrete investigation lead. It does not establish how those penalties entered this EPA computation or explain the full red-zone result.
- **Candidate mechanism only:** compressed field space may change the value of pressure timing, coverage, run fits and offensive play choices. No evidence yet establishes that mechanism for Seattle.

**Proposed bounded descriptive reconstruction:** inventory every qualified SEA W1–W4 red-zone possession and relevant plays; preserve game identity, field position before entry/at drive start, turnover-created short fields, down/distance, score/time, personnel, rush/pass choice, credited versus nullified events and possession-extending penalties. Add pressure/coverage/run-fit attribution only where qualified witnesses exist. Distinguish an EPA/play denominator from red-zone trips and TDs per trip.

**Support/challenge criteria for the descriptive thesis:** recurring comparable breakdowns across games would support a narrower situational weakness hypothesis; concentration in a few unusual possessions would narrow the broad claim. Missing functional evidence requires abstention from assigning responsibility. Personnel, opponent and game-state differences remain competing explanations. Any prospective Q2 criteria must be frozen separately before their outcome window.
