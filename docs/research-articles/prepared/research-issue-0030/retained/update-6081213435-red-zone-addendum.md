**Interpretation to test:**
- Strong results outside the red zone and poor results inside it are compatible: the subsets concern different situations and have very different sample sizes.
- Twenty-one qualifying plays is a small, possession-clustered population. A few consequential outcomes can move the rank materially. This warrants investigation, not a persistent bottom-two ability label or an unsupported confidence interval.
- EPA identifies outcome deterioration within the provider's model. It cannot assign causal responsibility to the secondary, front, scheme or individual defenders.
- Existing W4 evidence of four 15-yard penalties on the Chargers' two touchdown drives makes penalty/possession extension a concrete investigation lead. It does not establish how those penalties entered this EPA computation or explain the full red-zone result.
- **Candidate mechanism only:** compressed field space may change the value of pressure timing, coverage, run fits and offensive play choices. No evidence yet establishes that mechanism for Seattle.

**Proposed bounded descriptive reconstruction:** inventory every qualified SEA W1–W4 red-zone possession and relevant plays; preserve game identity, field position before entry/at drive start, turnover-created short fields, down/distance, score/time, personnel, rush/pass choice, credited versus nullified events and possession-extending penalties. Add pressure/coverage/run-fit attribution only where qualified witnesses exist. Distinguish an EPA/play denominator from red-zone trips and TDs per trip.

**Support/challenge criteria for the descriptive thesis:** recurring comparable breakdowns across games would support a narrower situational weakness hypothesis; concentration in a few unusual possessions would narrow the broad claim. Missing functional evidence requires abstention from assigning responsibility. Personnel, opponent and game-state differences remain competing explanations. Any prospective Q2 criteria must be frozen separately before their outcome window.

This addendum records a source lead and test specification. It does not admit the source, authenticate point-in-time availability, resolve historical comparisons, or activate reconstruction/model execution. All existing ownership and stop boundaries remain in force.
