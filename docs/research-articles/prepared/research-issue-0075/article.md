### Matchup questions

#### Can Tampa Bay keep Bucky and the run game effective

Tampa Bay's preferred route to a workable offense is evident, but Atlanta's run defense presents a different test from Carolina. Last week's rushing total also grew in a game Tampa led 33–6 at halftime. Sustained early-down gains would let Mayfield avoid repeatedly solving long-yardage situations; stalled runs would make protection and receiver execution more consequential. Evidence card Q1 preserves the distinction between a strong rushing outcome and repeatable control of ordinary downs.

#### Who benefits if Tampa Bay throws more

Egbuka's absence creates an opportunity to investigate redistribution. The last game favored Godwin in official targets, while Hurst remained involved enough to warrant observation without establishing a dependable share. More team passes could benefit Godwin, Johnson, Otton and Irving simultaneously; the available targets cannot all be assigned to Hurst.

Protection is a separate question. Goedeke's status and Mayfield's execution matter before an apparent weakness in Atlanta's pass-yardage defense can become usable receiving production. Cards Q2 and Q3 keep those claims separate.

#### Can Bijan contribute when rushing is difficult

Brian Robinson's absence supports a concentrated backfield, but Bijan's route to production includes receiving and cannot be reduced to rushing efficiency. Herbert participated when Brian was absent last week. The key distinction is between Atlanta generating useful team opportunities and Bijan receiving those opportunities. Cards Q4a and Q4b separately examine the rushing interaction and allocation.

#### Can Penix sustain distribution and Pitts sustain valuable opportunities

The five-start Penix window is more relevant than Atlanta's full-season quarterback average. Tampa Bay's recent sacks make disruption credible, while Atlanta's Week 7 protection is meaningful counterevidence.

Pitts needs his own test. His recent touchdowns and Tampa Bay's reported vulnerability to tight ends justify attention, but neither establishes a large receiving workload. Winfield's expected return changes the personnel context without proving who will cover Pitts. Cards Q5a and Q5b separate quarterback conditions from tight-end participation.

## 5. Interpretation and assessment

### How the game could develop

**Working scenario:** Both teams initially try to preserve their ground attacks. If those gains become difficult, Atlanta has a plausible route through Penix distributing to London, the tight ends and Bijan. Tampa Bay needs enough rushing success and clean passing possessions to sustain drives. This is a qualitative working explanation with moderate uncertainty.

**Tampa Bay alternative:** The front disrupts Penix, the offense gains advantageous field position, and Irving helps extend possessions. Receiver totals can remain limited even in a successful Tampa Bay performance. A repeat of last week's turnover-supported score is possible but should not be assumed.

**Atlanta alternative:** Penix handles pressure and creates an early lead. Tampa Bay then needs more dropbacks; its protection and receiving allocation become the central tests. A larger passing pool can increase opportunities without improving efficiency.

Availability can change these paths: Goedeke affects Tampa's protection options, Gainwell affects its backfield rotation, and Atlanta's questionable defenders affect its communication and secondary depth. Confirmed activity still does not establish full workload.

### Fantasy opportunity

- **Irving:** A strong workload case can coexist with a harder rushing matchup. Gainwell's status affects the rotation; an absence would not establish that every reserve touch transfers to Irving.

- **Bijan:** Receiving involvement provides another way to contribute when the ground game is inefficient. Brian being out strengthens the workload argument without establishing exclusive scoring-area access.

- **Godwin:** The clearest recent Tampa Bay receiving involvement. The sample is small and the passing pool may change substantially.

- **Hurst and Johnson:** Useful redistribution tests. Hurst needs stronger route access and repeated target earning; Johnson's recent touchdown does not settle the hierarchy.

- **London:** The established passing reference point remains relevant if Penix handles Tampa's disruption.

- **Pitts:** Current scoring-area promise with meaningful dependence on touchdowns. Track participation and opportunities even if the next box score contains another score.

These are implications of the conditional game paths, rather than personal roster decisions.
