## 3. Baseline and alternatives

#### 1. Freeze the starting beliefs before later outcomes

- **Operator hypothesis:** Black and Johnson are both high-value, potentially “A-tier” handcuffs. This is the operator's qualitative assessment, not an adopted TIBER grade.
- **H1 — replacement runner:** each is the likely first replacement as lead runner if McCaffrey / Walker is unavailable. This is an inference from current deployment, not an observed full-game replacement assignment.
- **H2 — passing involvement:** Johnson currently has stronger NFL evidence of an existing passing-down role. If Walker is absent, an expanded rushing-and-receiving role is plausible; its size and Smith's involvement remain unknown.
- **H3 — Black's trusted assignments:** Black has demonstrated competitive-drive and short-yardage trust. His lead-rushing case is credible; inheriting McCaffrey's receiving workload is unestablished.
- **Provisional full-PPR ordering at intake:** Johnson slightly ahead of Black, with a small, uncertain separation. Neither is an automatic cut on the evidence inspected.
- A high-value handcuff need not inherit every starter responsibility. A sufficient rushing/short-yardage role could support fantasy starts even with limited receiving.

The initial conversation put too much weight on Johnson's zero Week 4 carries. Passing-situation evidence changed the assessment before subsequent outcomes. Retain this reasoning change rather than retrospectively portraying a single unwavering opinion.

**Role witnesses inspected:**
- Team-published depth charts list Black second behind McCaffrey, ahead of Jordan James, and Johnson second behind Walker, ahead of Brashard Smith.
- Fantasy Life's Week 1 recap reports Black played five snaps on the opening drive. His involvement was not exclusively late-game cleanup.
- Matt Maiocco's Week 4 reporting describes Black receiving consecutive third-and-one / fourth-and-one rushing assignments against Denver in a 3–3 game. The fourth-down conversion followed an errant snap. This is short-yardage trust evidence, not proof of goal-line succession.
- San Francisco's official Week 4 inactive list includes James. His official season log records four carries for 15 yards through Week 5; availability must be distinguished from head-to-head deployment.
- Arrowhead Pride's PFF-based Week 4 review reports Johnson playing 17 offensive snaps, 12 on pass plays, versus two offensive snaps for Smith. A pass-play snap is not necessarily a route. The review identifies Johnson assuming passing-down work.

## 7. Next checkpoint and closeout

#### 3. Future observation plan

Keep Black and Johnson as the fixed cases; retain James and Smith as competing-back context. Dowdle and Mike Washington are outside this pilot unless explicitly added later with their own dated baseline. Do not add only successful later handcuffs.

For Weeks 6–18, when revisiting:
1. Record starter and backup availability, injury timing, roster competition and reason for absence. Separate full-game starter absence from midgame exits, ordinary rest, coach decisions and Week 18 rest.
2. For full-game absences, record who leads the team's RB carries, ties and committee splits. This is the primary observable test of the lead-runner expectation. If available, record shares of RB carries and team dropbacks with routes; track QB/FB/WR work separately.
3. For midgame exits, use only plays after the documented exit/return, identify game state and disclose the smaller denominator. Do not pool them silently with full games.
4. Track routes, protection, third downs, two-minute possessions, and carries inside the 20/10/5 where actually evidenced. These are nested field zones, not additive buckets. Missing / unassessed is not zero.
5. Record fantasy production separately from role: points, rushing/receiving contribution, TD dependence and opponent/game context. An expanded role can produce a poor fantasy game; a long TD can produce points without broad role inheritance.
6. Keep healthy-starter games as deployment context. Ordinary substitution is evidence about trust, not a causal demonstration of the no-starter counterfactual.
