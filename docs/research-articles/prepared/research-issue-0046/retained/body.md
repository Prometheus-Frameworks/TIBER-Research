## Source-backed baseline

### Player and capability context

- Houston selected Noel 79th overall in the 2025 draft. His final Iowa State season produced 80 receptions, 1,194 yards and eight touchdowns; the Texans also report a 4.39-second forty. Those are reasons to investigate his upside, not evidence of a current NFL target entitlement. [S1, S3]
- In 2025, he recorded 26 receptions, 292 yards and two receiving touchdowns across 17 games. Through six 2026 games, the official team profile records 17 receptions, 334 yards and two touchdowns, plus four rushes for 26 yards. His 2026 receiving yardage has already exceeded his rookie total, with substantially fewer receptions. [S1]
- His offseason right-pinkie injury led to a temporary non-football-injury designation; Houston reinstated him August 7. This historical injury report is not a current absence. Noel is absent from the inspected October 21 injury list. [S6, S10]
- No independent visual film review has been completed for this case. Route-running versatility, releases, coverage adjustments, ball skills and yards-after-catch capability remain questions to inspect rather than conclusions drawn from athletic testing or highlights.

## Competing hypotheses

These explanations can coexist. “Targets rise with Dell active” is an informative observation; one game cannot isolate Dell's causal effect from Harrison's integration, availability, personnel, opponent or game script.

| Hypothesis | Evidence that strengthens it | Evidence that weakens it |
|---|---|---|
| **H1 — Emerging durable target earner.** Noel's skill translates into a larger ordinary receiving role. | Routes hold or expand as Dell actually participates; targets and team target share increase; opportunities occur across possessions and normal game situations. Repetition after the bye strengthens the claim further. | Route access contracts in comparable healthy conditions; target earning remains limited despite sustained route access. A touchdown alone does not rescue the hypothesis. |
| **H2 — Explosive complementary receiver.** Noel remains useful on modest volume without becoming a dependable weekly fantasy starter. | Roughly the existing 3–5-target range persists, with production driven by a few long plays or touchdowns. | A recurring broader role and sustained target growth emerge, including useful games without exceptional efficiency. |
| **H3 — Competition limits opportunity.** Dell's return and/or Harrison's integration compress Noel's assignments. | Noel loses routes, ordinary passing-down work or credited target share as those receivers gain meaningful participation. | Noel retains or grows his role alongside them. Improved coexistence can weaken a simple blanket-dilution explanation. |
| **H4 — Apparent growth is context-dependent.** Extra work comes mainly from exceptional team passing volume, another absence, limited Dell deployment or unusual game situations. | Raw targets rise while target share stays flat/falls; work clusters late; another receiver/TE is unavailable; Dell plays only sparingly. | Growth persists across ordinary game conditions, healthier competition and subsequent games. |

## Prospective Week 7 signals

The following are **research heuristics selected before the game**, not projections or validated statistical cutoffs.

- **Stronger add-interest signal:** six or more targets, which would exceed every prior 2026 game, together with maintained/increased route access and a higher share of credited team targets. Dell must actually participate meaningfully for the proposed competition test to be informative.
- **Mixed/hold signal:** four or five targets with stable routes and an encouraging distribution of assignments. This can be progress, but it remains within the existing target range.
- **Weaker near-term signal:** fewer opportunities alongside a contracting role as competition returns, even if a single explosive play produces a strong fantasy total.
- **Incomplete test:** Dell does not play, is used only briefly, another major receiving option is absent, or comparable usage evidence is unavailable. Record those conditions rather than declaring H1 confirmed or rejected.
- A poor box score with expanding opportunity can be encouraging; another efficient scoring game without expanding opportunity can leave the central question unresolved.

## What each follow-up records

For Noel, Dell, Harrison, Collins and relevant supporting receivers/TEs, collect only evidence actually available:

1. Actual game/activation status, injuries and any documented workload limits; game identity, completion status and opponent.
2. Team passing opportunities, offensive snaps, player routes, targets, receptions, yards and touchdowns. Keep missing fields unknown.
3. **Target share:** player targets divided by total credited team targets, with the source and denominator stated. Pass-attempt share is a different measure and must be labeled separately. **Targets per route:** targets divided by player routes when both are verified. Preserve source-specific route-participation definitions.
4. Early/late-game distribution, ordinary possessions versus unusual situations, alignments/personnel and short/intermediate/deep or red-zone work where supported. Record Dell's actual routes and Harrison's workload rather than using an active tag as a full-strength competition indicator.
5. Separate route access, target earning and conversion efficiency. Do not label targets “earned” in a causal sense solely because the ball arrived.
6. If suitable footage is inspected, include untargeted routes and source/timecodes; distinguish visible execution from interpretation. Highlight packages and interview-page descriptions do not establish a representative film sample, designed progression or first-read status.
7. For every hypothesis: strengthened, weakened or unresolved; strongest alternative explanation; material source conflicts; what observation would change the assessment next.
