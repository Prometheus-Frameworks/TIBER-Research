- **Prescott and Lamb** take the Dallas side. DAL −6 with a 51.5 total implies about 28.75 points for Dallas. Lamb is the top-salaried receiver on that side.
- **Downs and Warren** are the Indianapolis side of the stack. Indianapolis is a 6-point home underdog, so a trailing script favors their passing game over a Colts running back. I chose these two over Jonathan Taylor ($7,500) because of game script and cost. This is a deliberate double bring-back.
- **McCaffrey** belongs to the slate's highest implied team: SF −10 against LV with a 51.5 total, about 30.75 points. His mild hamstring strain (Week 7) was reported as expected to be ready for Week 9, after San Francisco's Week 8 bye. His running and receiving workload is what I am paying for.
- **Stevenson** is reported in a workhorse role while TreVeyon Henderson recovers from a high-ankle sprain. A separate November 2 report lists Henderson as deactivated for illness in Week 8. NE is −3 at home against GB, with a 46.5 total.
- **Worthy** is an inexpensive way into Kansas City's implied total of about 28 points (KC −9.5, total 46.5).
- **Sutton** is the salary-fitting WR3. DEN is −3 at CAR with a 43.5 total.
- **The Eagles DST** was the cheapest defense with a supporting reason. It is a home favorite in the lowest-total game, and the Giants' implied total is about 18.5.

## Strongest counterargument

The case against this lineup rests on **availability and role, not game environment**:

1. **McCaffrey carries the most fragile risk.** At $8,000 he is my most expensive non-WR. The evidence is a pre-bye "mild strain, should be ready" report, not an official Week 9 practice status.
2. **The cheap slots have the weakest evidence.** I did not retrieve current-season role data for Sutton, Worthy or Downs.
3. **The Indianapolis bring-back reverses if the script flips.** If the Colts lead or simply run Taylor, Downs and Warren both lose volume together. That would be a correlated failure.

The Week 8 lesson applies directly here: role labels that sound plausible can fail.
