# Five questions that shape the game

## 1 Can Arizona give Brissett usable time

**Q1 evidence card — links Q2a, Q2b and Q3.** The working explanation is that line disruption constrains the passing game before receiver talent can matter. The credible alternative is that quick throws, chips and formation choices keep the offense functional.

**Evidence:** Paris Johnson Jr. and Isaiah Adams are on IR. The [Friday team account](https://www.azcardinals.com/news/love-ing-the-running-game-and-friday-before-the-cowboys) describes a possible Conner move from right guard to center if Froholdt cannot play, creating another replacement at guard. The absence and designation in the current-state table are established; the final five-man lineup is not. Quinnen Williams returned to full Friday practice; Clark's availability remains a separate uncertainty.

**Interpretation:** protection is Arizona's main failure risk, not a reason to declare every receiving opportunity poor. Dallas's season run allowance also argues against treating this as an automatically dominant defense. A completed short pass can protect Brissett without generating a useful first down.

**Sample and metric fit:** pregame personnel plus Weeks 1–7 team outcomes; no player-alignment, pressure-rate or block-responsibility sample. Sacks do not measure all pressures. No claim of a fixed Quinnen-versus-one-guard matchup.

**Confidence and revision:** high in the reported personnel problems; moderate in protection being a central constraint, low in predicting its size. Froholdt's actual availability and a settled replacement arrangement could improve the view. Early interior disruption despite extra help would strengthen it. **Watch:** whether Arizona can complete on-time passes beyond immediate outlets; broadcast-visible, while protection responsibility requires film. **Status: mixed.**

## 2 Does Wilson gain useful opportunity after Brooks

**Q2a evidence card — route access and health; links Q1 and Q2b.** A larger usable route role is plausible, but ribs, extra blocking assignments or a committee can prevent it. [Arizona Sports' Friday report](https://arizonasports.com/arizona-cardinals-injury-report/cardinals-cowboys-week-8-injury-report-jeremiyah-will-play) says Wilson is expected to play, with no game designation despite limited practices. That establishes availability expectation, not full health or a workload guarantee.

The [official Denver snap account](https://www.azcardinals.com/news/defensive-lineman-l-j-collier-still-a-cardinal-still-getting-more-snaps) records Wilson on 73 of 81 offensive snaps, McBride on 75 and Brooks on 52. Wilson's calculated 90.1% offensive-snap share is already high. There is more scope for a change in route assignments and target decisions than a simple assumption that he gains every vacated snap.

**Sample and limit:** one Week 7 game, before Brooks's absence; snaps are not routes. Current depth-chart WR labels do not establish X/Z alignment on actual plays. **Confidence:** high in reported status and snaps; low in projected route redistribution. **Revision trigger:** Sunday participation restrictions or a clear new receiver/TE rotation. **Watch:** Wilson's presence and releases in ordinary passing situations; exact route participation requires charting. **Status: unresolved future allocation.**

**Q2b evidence card — target allocation; links Q2a and Q3.** The working explanation is a McBride-led distribution with Wilson still the main WR. The alternative is a more concentrated Wilson role after the latest personnel change.

Our [retained Week 7 study](https://github.com/Prometheus-Frameworks/TIBER-Research/issues/47#issuecomment-6417265640) records Wilson at **22 of 124 credited team targets in Weeks 5–7, 17.7%**, compared with **30 of 85, 35.3%, in Weeks 3–4**. Against Denver, McBride had 13 of 44 credited targets, Wilson eight, and Brooks and Bourne six each. These are retained ESPN/FootballDB counts, not a fresh verification of those external boxes today.

[Arizona's Brooks report](https://www.azcardinals.com/news/jalen-brooks-season-ending-injury-creates-another-challenge-for-cardinals) attributes a committee replacement plan to LaFleur. A returning receiving-capable RB and multiple TEs are credible competing destinations. Brooks played through his injury in Week 7; his absence is a new prospective condition.

**Metric fit:** summed credited targets divided by summed team credited targets; not pass attempts, routes or first reads. Two- and three-game windows differ in opponents and personnel and do not identify a trade effect. The original study's conflicting denominator conventions remain separate.

**Confidence:** moderate in the retained counts and established distribution; low in the size of a coming change. **Revision trigger:** Wilson gaining a sustained share of ordinary passing opportunities with comparable route access, rather than merely scoring once. **Watch:** early targets to McBride, Wilson, RBs and secondary receivers. **Status: supported baseline, unresolved new distribution.**

## 3 What does Love actually reclaim

**Q3 evidence card — links Q1 and Q2b.** A healthier Love could give Arizona both rushing help and a receiving outlet. The strongest alternative is a three-player rotation behind the same damaged line.

Love practiced fully Friday and has no game designation. His [October 16 coach explanation](https://www.azcardinals.com/news/jeremiyah-love-ruled-out-against-rams) specifically linked the limited receiving role to ankle-impaired cutting and separation, describing more passing involvement as a health-contingent plan. The return creates a test of that plan; it does not prove the role has arrived.

Knight played 43 offensive snaps to Allgeier's 38 against Denver. That recent rotation shows the alternatives have real involvement, although it cannot tell us the split with Love back. [Official snap account](https://www.azcardinals.com/news/defensive-lineman-l-j-collier-still-a-cardinal-still-getting-more-snaps).

**Sample and exclusions:** pre-injury coaching intent, two missed games and one detailed replacement-rotation game; mixed personnel regimes. No current routes, pass-protection grades or goal-line allocation estimate. Snap shares are not carry or target shares.

**Interpretation:** Love can help the offense while competing with Wilson for targets. Improved drive survival could benefit multiple players, but the same fixed pass volume cannot be allocated fully to all of them. **Confidence:** high in return expectation; low in role size or efficiency. **Revision trigger:** real passing-down routes and intended targets, versus protection-only assignments or extended sideline stretches. **Watch:** who plays on third down and in scoring territory; routes versus chips need closer replay. **Status: unresolved workload.**

Arizona's separate finishing problem is real: the [Friday team account](https://www.azcardinals.com/news/love-ing-the-running-game-and-friday-before-the-cowboys) reports one touchdown in ten red-zone possessions across the last two games. That is not a Love-specific opportunity count or proof that his return fixes it. A causal red-zone diagnosis is deferred.

## 4 Can Dallas sustain the rushing improvement

**Q4 evidence card — links Q5b.** Dallas can try to use Williams to stay out of difficult passing downs. The alternative is that the Philadelphia result was an opponent-specific peak and Dallas again needs its passing game to carry the offense.

The [official Williams log](https://www.dallascowboys.com/team/players-roster/javonte-williams/) has **25 carries for 155 yards in Week 7**. His first six games total **85 carries for 317 yards: 3.73 per carry**, calculated by subtracting Week 7 from 110 carries and 472 season yards. The jump to 6.2 in one game is meaningful recent performance, not a stable expectation.

Arizona's run allowance in the baseline and Nolen's absence provide reasons to test repeatability. They do not prove a specific run concept or blocking matchup will work. The [Friday Arizona account](https://www.azcardinals.com/news/love-ing-the-running-game-and-friday-before-the-cowboys) attributes a 66.4% Dallas called-pass rate to Next Gen Stats, compared with 38 official team rushes against Philadelphia; the called-pass definition and raw denominator are not exposed, so these are not combined into one rate.

**Metric fit:** official carries and yards; no success-rate, box-count or run-concept analysis. No averaging unequal weekly rates. **Confidence:** high in the observed rushing contrast; moderate in a viable Dallas rushing path, low in a repeat of 155 yards. **Revision trigger:** repeated early-down losses or Arizona consistently forcing long passing downs. **Watch:** whether ordinary first-down runs preserve manageable second and third downs. **Status: mixed.**

## 5 Can Dak exploit the passing matchup

**Q5a evidence card — Pickens availability and role; links Q5b.** The working scenario has Lamb and Pickens available together. The alternative is a limited or absent Pickens changing both distribution and defensive attention.

The [Dallas Friday update](https://www.dallascowboys.com/news/updates-october-2026) lists Pickens questionable with shoulder/hamstring issues while attributing an expectation that he plays to Schottenheimer. **Expected to play is not confirmed active.** Melton and Wingard are questionable for Arizona. Their active status and workload cannot be inferred from practice participation.

**Interpretation:** Lamb's established involvement is the clearer foundation. Pickens has upside conditional on his role; Flournoy could gain opportunity if Pickens is limited, but no one-for-one target transfer is assumed. **Evidence needed:** actual participation, receiver rotation and comparable targets, not just names on the active list. **Confidence:** high in designations, low in workload effect. **Watch:** whether Pickens runs normal routes through successive series; exact route share needs charting. **Status: unresolved workload.**

**Q5b evidence card — protection against disguise; links Q4 and Q5a.** Rallis and Baker can challenge Dallas's identification and protection. The alternative is that the pressure looks fail to generate disruption and Dallas finds throws downfield.

The [Cowboys' October 30 analysis](https://www.dallascowboys.com/news/here-we-goooo-3-things-the-cowboys-must-do-to-beat-the-cardinals) reports pressure on **16 of 27 Prescott third-and-long dropbacks**, while citing **31.4%** Arizona pressure in those situations. The third-and-long threshold, full provider definition and Arizona numerator/denominator are not exposed. These remain attributed situational figures, not independently reproduced matchup probabilities. The article mislabels the Packers meeting as Week 7; the official schedule places it in Week 6.

**Interpretation:** pressure disguise is an Arizona counter-scenario, not proof of strong aggregate pressure generation. Early-down Dallas success reduces exposure; a weak rushing day increases it. Exact defensive alignments and individual assignments remain unknown.

**Confidence:** moderate in the attributed vulnerability; low in its transfer to this game. **Revision trigger:** Dallas consistently identifying pressure and avoiding negative plays, or Arizona repeatedly producing free rushers. **Watch:** third-down protection and Baker's location without assuming every line-of-scrimmage appearance is a blitz. **Status: mixed.**

# Game scenarios and fantasy implications

**Leading scenario:** Dallas sustains enough ordinary drives to make Arizona chase. Williams helps preserve workable downs, and Prescott benefits if Pickens can contribute normally. Arizona can still accumulate completions, especially through McBride, while protection and finishing limit what those possessions become.

**Credible alternative:** Arizona's protection is functional, Love supplies a useful underneath answer, and Dallas's defensive weaknesses let possessions continue. Arizona converts more of its scoring trips while its pressure disguises interrupt Dallas. This branch becomes more plausible if Froholdt plays effectively, Clark is limited or absent, or Dallas's rushing improvement disappears.

These are analyst scenarios, not calibrated forecasts. A Dallas passing-volume spike and a dominant ball-control rushing script are different paths; do not assume their player ceilings all occur together.

# Three things to watch

1. **Arizona protection versus functional passing:** can Brissett reach his intended timing, including on intermediate throws, without every useful snap requiring extra help?

2. **Arizona's first few passing rotations:** distinguish Wilson's routes, McBride's targets and Love's receiving use. A broadcast can suggest a pattern; exact rates require charting.

3. **Dallas's down-and-distance:** does Williams keep the offense on schedule, and can Dak solve disguised pressure when it does face long passing downs?

The most useful pregame revisions are the actual Froholdt, Clark, Pickens, Melton and Wingard statuses. Even confirmed active status will not settle their workload.
