# Question selection and the selection log

Choose questions after the compact two-team baseline and before the main question-specific evidence collection. Let the evidence change the agenda, but preserve the original decision and record later changes with their timestamp and reason. The log is a research artifact, not a claim that the eventual outcome was known.

## Candidate test

For each candidate, specify the offensive action, possible defensive response and consequence. Write both forks:

- If **A** is true, what changes in the game interpretation, player opportunity, uncertainty or what to watch?
- If **B** is true, what changes instead?

If neither answer changes a meaningful conclusion or viewing priority, defer or omit it. A known absence can be an important baseline fact even when its consequences, rather than the absence itself, need investigation.

Assess each candidate using judgment, not a numerical score:

| Consideration | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Consequence | Could either answer change a central scenario or opportunity? |
| Exposure | Will the interaction recur, or arise in a small number of critical situations? |
| Uncertainty | Is it unresolved, contested or sensitive to a change in personnel or role? |
| Evidence potential | Is there a feasible source or calculation that could distinguish the explanations? |
| Distinct contribution | Does the question add information beyond the other selected questions? |

Give a **high**, **medium** or **low** priority and a short reason. Separate importance from researchability: keep an important but currently unanswerable question as an explicit uncertainty and use conditional scenarios. Do not let convenient data determine the research agenda.

## Linked questions are not automatically duplicates

Use separate IDs and evidence cards when questions need different sources, measurements or conditions, even if their outcomes interact. Link parent, sibling or dependency IDs and explain the relationship. Shared narrative placement is not shared evidentiary support. Examples:

- Pass rush alignment, pressure and containment require rush-side, rush-count and protection evidence; a receiver's availability and routes require injury, participation and deployment evidence. Neither answers the other.
- A defense's scoring-area access and one player's share of opportunities require team and individual denominators respectively.
- A player's ability on routes, the offense's route deployment and the quarterback's target decisions can coexist in tension; none automatically resolves the others.

Combine candidates only if the same evidence and plausible answers truly answer both. Normally choose three to five main questions, with at least one for each offense. If distinct material subquestions would exceed that number, group them under a main theme **without** erasing their separate IDs, cards, alternatives or unknowns.

## Log requirements

Use the log template in [templates](templates.md). For **each selected question** record its ID, question, why it matters, priority and reason, A/B consequences, planned distinguishing evidence, and links to related questions. For **each deferred candidate** record the question, reason and what would make it worth revisiting. Record material changes in an append-only dated decision entry, identifying the new evidence and the affected IDs. A retrospective reconstruction must be labeled as such; never present it as a contemporaneous selection.
